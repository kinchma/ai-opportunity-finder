Section 608 of the Clean Air Act makes it illegal to knowingly release refrigerant while installing, servicing, repairing or disposing of air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment. The rule covers both ozone-depleting refrigerants and their substitutes, such as HFCs and HFOs.

Anyone who services or disposes of equipment containing refrigerant, or buys refrigerant for stationary equipment, must hold a Section 608 technician certification from an EPA-approved program. Refrigerant may be sold only to certified technicians, and only for the equipment types their certification covers. The business must keep a copy of each technician's certificate on file until three years after that technician stops working.

Before opening or disposing of equipment, technicians must recover refrigerant down to the required evacuation levels using certified recovery or recycling equipment. Recovered refrigerant can be resold only after it has been reclaimed by an EPA-certified reclaimer, with narrow exceptions.

When appliances holding 5 to 50 pounds of refrigerant are disposed of, records must show the location, the recovery date and the refrigerant type for each appliance, the amount of each refrigerant recovered per month, and how much was sent for reclamation or destruction, to whom and when.

Under Section 608 itself, leak inspection and repair rules for larger appliances (comfort cooling of 50 pounds or more, with a 10% leak-rate threshold) apply only to ozone-depleting refrigerants. HFC equipment of 15 pounds or more is covered by a separate 2026 leak repair rule (see the EPA ER&R note). Technicians must give owners their service invoices and leak test results.

What this means for operations: every service ticket that touches refrigerant should capture the technician's certification, the refrigerant type and amount added or recovered, and the equipment it came from. Certification records need tracking and renewal reminders, and disposal and recovery logs must be monthly and complete. AI can check tickets for missing refrigerant data and prepare the logs, but a certified technician remains responsible for the work.
