Since January 1, 2023, residential central air conditioners and heat pumps are rated with new test metrics (SEER2, EER2 and HSPF2) and must meet higher minimum efficiencies. The national minimum applies by date of manufacture. For split-system air conditioners there are also regional minimums that apply by date of installation: roughly 13.4 SEER2 in the North and 14.3 SEER2 in the Southeast and Southwest (for units under 45,000 Btu/h), with an added EER2 requirement in the Southwest. Heat pumps follow national minimums everywhere: 14.3 SEER2 and 7.5 HSPF2 for split systems.

DOE defines installation as connecting the refrigerant lines or electrical supply to make the system operational. In the Southeast (including Texas, Florida, Georgia, the Carolinas, Virginia, Maryland, Delaware and DC) and the Southwest (Arizona, California, Nevada and New Mexico), only models certified to the regional standard may be installed after January 1, 2023. A model qualifies only if every indoor-outdoor combination in it meets the standard, so the pairing of the outdoor unit with its coil matters, not just the outdoor unit.

What this means for operations: proposals and installs must use matched, certified combinations that meet the standard for the region where the job is. A contractor working near a regional boundary, or holding older inventory, needs a check that each quoted combination is compliant for that address. Recording the exact model combination and its certified ratings on every install also supports warranty registration, rebates and later service.
