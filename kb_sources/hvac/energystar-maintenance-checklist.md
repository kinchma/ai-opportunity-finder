ENERGY STAR recommends an annual pre-season check-up by a contractor: the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall, because contractors get busy once the hot and cold seasons arrive. Homeowners should inspect, clean or change air filters monthly, since a dirty filter raises energy use and can lead to costly repairs or early failure.

A general check-up should cover thermostat settings, tightening electrical connections and measuring motor voltage and current, lubricating moving parts, checking the condensate drain on the air conditioner, furnace or heat pump, and checking the controls through a full start-up, run and shut-down cycle.

Cooling visits should add cleaning the indoor and outdoor coils, checking and adjusting the refrigerant charge, and cleaning and adjusting the blower for proper airflow. Heating visits should add checking gas or oil connections, gas pressure, burner combustion and the heat exchanger, since a dirty burner or a cracked heat exchanger is a safety and efficiency problem.

What this means for operations: the checklist is a ready-made standard for a maintenance visit and a maintenance agreement. Seasonal demand makes scheduling the key constraint, so a contractor benefits from booking tune-ups ahead of each season, sending renewal and visit reminders, recording each checklist item and reading on a mobile form, and turning findings (a weak capacitor, a cracked heat exchanger, a dirty coil) into follow-up quotes. AI can draft reminders, summarize visit notes and flag findings that need a quote, while technicians perform and sign off the inspection.
