Since January 1, 2026, owners or operators and certified technicians must follow leak repair rules for equipment holding 15 pounds or more of a refrigerant that contains an HFC or an HFC substitute with a global warming potential above 53. Residential and light commercial air conditioners and heat pumps are excluded, but chillers, some variable refrigerant flow systems and commercial refrigeration are covered, so the rule matters for a contractor's commercial accounts.

Leak-rate thresholds are 10% for comfort cooling and most other appliances, 20% for commercial refrigeration and 30% for industrial process refrigeration. The leak rate must be calculated every time refrigerant is added. A leak above the threshold must be repaired within 30 days of adding refrigerant (120 days if an industrial process shutdown is needed), and every repair needs an initial and a follow-up verification test.

After a leak above the threshold, inspections are required: quarterly for large commercial and industrial systems of 500 pounds or more, and annually for smaller ones and for comfort cooling, until the leak rate stays below the threshold for the required period. If the owner decides to retrofit or retire the equipment instead of repairing it, a written plan is due within 30 days and the work is generally due within a year.

An appliance of 15 pounds or more that leaks 125% or more of its full charge in a calendar year must be reported to EPA by March 1 of the following year. Records must be kept for three years.

What this means for operations: a contractor serving commercial customers needs each covered appliance's full charge on file, every refrigerant addition logged with a leak-rate calculation, and repair deadlines and verification tests tracked per site. These are calendar and paperwork tasks that are easy to miss by hand and well suited to automated reminders and checks, with the owner and technician still deciding on repairs.
