The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25C) covered improvements made through December 31, 2025. Legislation in 2025 ended it for later installations, so equipment placed in service in 2026 does not qualify. Homeowners claim it for the year the property was installed, on Form 5695.

While it lasted, it paid 30% of cost: up to $600 each for qualifying central air conditioners and gas, propane or oil furnaces and boilers, and up to $2,000 a year for qualifying heat pumps and heat pump water heaters, with labor included. Equipment had to meet the Consortium for Energy Efficiency's highest non-advanced tier, come from a qualified manufacturer, and for 2025 the manufacturer's identification number (QMID) had to be reported on the return.

What this means for operations: contractor marketing, proposals and financing scripts that promise the federal heat pump or furnace credit are now out of date and should be removed or corrected; customers who installed in 2025 may still ask for paperwork (model numbers, efficiency tier, manufacturer identification) to claim it. Remaining incentives are mostly state, utility and federally funded state rebate programs, which vary by location and change often, so incentive information should be kept current and checked per address.
