The SBA lists the main benefits of AI tools for a small business as: spotting problems early, safeguarding data, analyzing business data, automating repeat tasks, creating content, brainstorming and improving customer service.

Its risk list is short and practical: a software vendor is responsible for its product, but free or general-purpose tools need a person to review output for ethics, security and accuracy; watch for intellectual-property infringement in generated content; do not enter sensitive data into tools you do not control; expect AI-written phishing aimed at the business; and expect some customers to push back on automated outreach.

The SBA recommends a short public statement disclosing how the business uses AI, even though no federal or state law requires one (as of the page's October 2026 update).

Good first uses are tasks that are high-volume, repetitive and rule-governed with a clear right answer, where a person checks the result: drafting routine replies, classifying and summarizing documents, extracting fields from forms and emails, and first-draft content. Decisions with legal, safety or financial consequences should stay with a person.
