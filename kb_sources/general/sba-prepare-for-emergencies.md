The SBA's emergency-preparedness sequence has three steps: assess the risks (a self-assessment covering hazards such as hurricanes, wildfires, flooding and cyberattacks), create a plan tailored to the operation and easy to reach (resources include the SBA Business Resilience Guide, the IRS disaster preparation guide, FEMA checklists and Ready.gov), and practice the plan with staff.

Business continuity for a small company comes down to a few questions: where are the records and backups, who can run payroll and billing if the owner is unavailable, which suppliers and customers must be called first, and how long the business can operate without its main system or site.

After a disaster the SBA offers low-interest disaster loans for damaged property and business assets, economic injury loans and mitigation assistance; applying is free and accepting an approved loan is optional.
