The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1, released January 26, 2023) is voluntary guidance for managing the risks of AI systems to individuals, organizations and society, and for building trustworthiness into how AI is designed, developed, used and evaluated. The framework PDF is at https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf. NIST stated in 2026 that AI RMF 1.0 is being revised.

The framework's core has four functions. Govern: set the policies, roles, risk tolerance and accountability for AI use across the organization. Map: understand the context, the intended use, who is affected and what could go wrong before building or buying. Measure: test and monitor the system with metrics that cover more than accuracy. Manage: prioritize and act on the measured risks, including the option not to deploy, and respond when something goes wrong.

Companion resources: the AI RMF Playbook (suggested actions for each function), the Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, July 26, 2024) covering risks specific to generative AI such as confabulation, data privacy, information integrity and harmful content, an AI RMF Roadmap and Crosswalks to other standards.

Applied to a small business, the framework translates into a short routine: name an owner for each AI use; write down what the tool is for and which data it may see; keep a person responsible for the decision, with review of outputs before they reach customers, employees or regulators; measure a few things (error rate on a sample, how often a person had to correct the tool, time saved); and keep a way to switch the tool off and recover.

Human oversight is strongest where the consequence of an error is high: safety-critical checks, hiring and firing, credit and pricing decisions, legal and tax filings, and anything that touches customers' personal data. Those are the uses to keep advisory, with a person making the final decision.
