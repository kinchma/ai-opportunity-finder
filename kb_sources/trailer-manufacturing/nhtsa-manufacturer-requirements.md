Trailers are motor vehicles under federal law (a vehicle that carries persons or property and is drawn by another motor vehicle), so a trailer manufacturer is a motor vehicle manufacturer with NHTSA obligations. NHTSA does not type-approve vehicles; the manufacturer self-certifies that each trailer complies with every applicable Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS). The handbook cites civil penalties of up to $27,168 per violation and $135,828 for a related series, as of its publication date.

Registration: submit manufacturer identification and a product description to NHTSA within 30 days of starting manufacture (49 CFR Part 566) and report business changes within 30 days. Submit VIN-deciphering information at least 60 days before the first vehicle with that VIN is offered for sale (49 CFR Part 565).

VINs have 17 characters without I, O or Q and a check digit in position 9. A low-volume manufacturer (fewer than 1,000 vehicles of a type per year) uses 9 as the third character of its World Manufacturer Identifier, which is obtained from SAE International. For trailers, positions 4 to 8 encode connection type, body type, length and axle configuration.

Certification label (49 CFR Part 567): permanently affixed on the forward half of the left side, readable from outside without moving any part of the trailer. It must show the manufacturer's name, month and year of manufacture, GVWR, the GAWR for each axle with tire size, rim size and cold inflation pressure, the vehicle type (TRAILER), the VIN and the statement that the vehicle conforms to all applicable FMVSS in effect on the date of manufacture. An incomplete-vehicle manufacturer must supply an Incomplete Vehicle Document.

Equipment standards that commonly apply to trailers include lamps, reflective devices and associated equipment (FMVSS 108), brake hoses (106) and brake fluid (116), tires and rims (FMVSS 119 and 120 for vehicles other than passenger cars), rear impact guards for heavier trailers (223 and 224) and glazing where used (205). The applicable set depends on trailer type and weight; confirm against 49 CFR Part 571. NHTSA's trailer brochure also points to 49 CFR Part 576 for retaining records of claims, complaints and reports.

Defects and recalls: a manufacturer that decides in good faith that a vehicle has a safety-related defect or noncompliance must notify NHTSA within five working days, notify owners and dealers (49 CFR Part 577) and usually provide the remedy at no charge. NHTSA can also order a recall.

What this means for operations: every trailer needs a build record that ties the VIN to its axles, tires, rims, lighting and brake components and to the label values; inspection sign-off and label verification are compliance steps, not just quality steps; and warranty claims, complaints and dealer reports must be kept and reviewable. AI can prepare and check this paperwork and flag gaps, but a responsible person certifies compliance.

Trade groups named by NHTSA for further technical guidance: the National Association of Trailer Manufacturers (trailers under 26,000 lb) and the Truck Trailer Manufacturers Association (over 26,000 lb).
