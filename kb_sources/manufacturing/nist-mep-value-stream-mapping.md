Value stream mapping (VSM) visualizes a manufacturing process and its information flows in one view. MEP describes it as often the first step in identifying waste and opportunities to streamline processes and reduce lead times.

VSM has four steps: identify the product value stream to map; develop the current-state map of materials, processes and information flow; diagnose problems, identify opportunities and define a future state; implement the improvements.

Mapping is done by a cross-functional team, which builds agreement and makes sure every area is considered. Teams choose which improvements to pursue using criteria such as biggest return for the effort, largest reduction in lead time or inventory, biggest impact on the customer, highest probability of success, visibility to stakeholders, new product or service lines, and volume.

The future-state map becomes the basis for other lean tools (continuous flow, kanban, setup reduction) and serves as a shared roadmap that aligns the organization around one improvement plan.

For an AI opportunity assessment, a current-state map is the best input: it shows where information is re-typed, where work waits for a decision or a missing part, and which steps are manual hand-offs between people, systems and paper. Those are the points where an assistant that extracts, classifies, matches or answers questions fits.
