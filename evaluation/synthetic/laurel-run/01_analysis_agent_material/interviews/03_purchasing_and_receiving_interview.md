# Simulated interview: purchasing and receiving

**Fictional disclosure:** This is a simulated, text-only interview for the fictional Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc. It is not a record of an actual conversation.

**Interviewee:** Devon Kersey, Materials and Purchasing Manager  
**Snapshot:** 2026-10-31  
**Topic:** Purchasing, receiving, and the related inventory and accounting handoffs  
**Intended duration:** Approximately 20 minutes, including natural pauses

## [00:00]

**Interviewer:** Devon, could you start with the part of the business you look after and how purchasing connects to the people around you?

**SME:** I’m responsible for purchasing and inventory records, and I report to Owen Bellamy, our plant manager. Jalen Sorrell is our buyer, and Cora Whitlock handles receiving and inventory. Both report to me. Mateo Renshaw, our shipping coordinator, also reports to me, although outgoing shipping isn’t the subject of the purchase-order and receipt steps we’re talking about.

**Interviewer:** When someone asks you what starts an order, is there a single signal you wait for?

**SME:** It’s a combination. Cora hand-counts six selected, frequently used SKU categories each week. She notes the counts on paper and updates the Excel inventory sheet. I look at what’s on that sheet, the open purchase orders, and what we’re likely to need in the near term. Then I use those inputs and my experience to decide the quantity. The stock sheet is our working count; it’s not the same thing as the list of what we expect to receive.

**Interviewer:** How do you keep those two ideas separate when you’re making the decision?

**SME:** The item and the unit matter. If I have axles physically on hand, that’s a count of axles. If an order for axles is still open, that’s an expected receipt against a purchase order, not axles already available to the floor. I need to look at both when I decide how much to buy, but I wouldn’t tell someone that an unreceived delivery is stock on hand. [Pause.] It can sound like a small distinction until someone needs the material.

**Interviewer:** Can you use a count from the records to make that concrete?

**SME:** On October 30, Cora counted 21 of SKU-0001, the Trailfield axle assembly. The stock sheet showed 21 before the count and 21 after it. That’s useful for that item at that count, but it’s not a reconciliation of the whole inventory. The selected count sample doesn’t tell me the total value of all stock. If you ask me for that value, I’m not sure.

**Interviewer:** What happens after you’ve reached a quantity?

**SME:** Jalen prepares the purchase order for my approval, including the quantity. I approve the PO and the quantity; after that, he assigns the next PO number and emails it to the supplier through Outlook. The Excel PO list keeps the supplier, item, quantity, delivered price, and PO reference. PO numbers have their own sequence. We also have an Excel supplier list and the separate inventory sheet. QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise is where supplier bills are entered; it isn’t the working shop-floor stock count.

## [04:00]

**Interviewer:** Let’s walk an actual purchase order from the records. Which one would you choose?

**SME:** PO-2026-0237, for SKU-0001 from Ridge Hollow Components LLC, supplier of record SUP-0001. It was ordered October 8 and all 50 axles on the line were received October 15. The price was $276 each delivered, excluding sales tax, so the line was $13,800 on that basis. The terms shown are net 30. That’s an order and a receipt in the sample; it doesn’t tell us when the bill was paid.

**Interviewer:** What exactly is included when you say an axle?

**SME:** It’s a 3,500-pound electric-braked spring axle assembly, with both drums and hubs. It has an 89-inch hub face, a 74-inch spring center and a five-on-4.5-inch bolt pattern. Springs and hangers are separate. I’d want the item and its full specification in mind, not just the phrase “3,500-pound axle.”

**Interviewer:** Why the extra detail if the rating sounds the same?

**SME:** The materials list also has SKU-0002, another 3,500-pound electric-braked spring axle, but that one has a 95-inch hub face and an 80-inch spring center. It’s listed with Fernbank Running Gear as supplier of record. Those dimensions are not interchangeable just because the rating sounds familiar. And an axle rating alone does not establish the payload of a finished trailer. The finished trailer’s certification and load labels are what need checking there.

**Interviewer:** Does the October order represent an unusual size?

**SME:** The regular Ridge Hollow release for SKU-0001 is 50 each monthly. The selected PO lines show 600 of those axles received during the reporting year. They’re selected lines for that SKU, not all our purchase orders or all our purchases. Northmere Metals Service has weekly releases for steel stock, so the axle rhythm isn’t a rule I would apply to every material.

**Interviewer:** If someone handed you just a line total, could you tell what was delivered?

**SME:** Not reliably. I’d need the item and the quantity, with the unit. A steel tube may be counted in lengths, a tire and wheel in assemblies, and the floor screws in boxes. For SKU-0012 the box contains 250 screws. The unit on the material record matters when you’re comparing the order to a receipt; a box is not one loose screw.

## [08:00]

**Interviewer:** Take me to the receiving bay. What does Cora do when a delivery arrives?

**SME:** She counts what comes in against the purchase order, records the receipt on paper and signs the packing slip. She updates the inventory sheet with what actually arrived. If the delivery is short or damaged, she tells Jalen before updating the sheet. A partial delivery is a partial receipt; we don’t record the entire order as received just because part of it reached the bay.

**Interviewer:** Suppose a PO calls for more than is on the truck. How would you explain the distinction to someone looking only at the PO list?

**SME:** I’d say the PO tells you what was ordered, while the receipt tells you what came in. Cora’s count is important because the ordered quantity isn’t proof that every unit arrived. A remaining expectation and an actual receipt are different pieces of information. The short delivery goes to Jalen before the sheet is updated. I’m describing the procedure there, not saying that the October Ridge Hollow line was short; that line was received in full.

**Interviewer:** And for damage, do you just put the ordered amount into the sheet and sort it out later?

**SME:** No. Cora tells Jalen about damage before updating the inventory sheet, and the receipt records what actually arrived rather than the whole order by assumption. As for the disposition of a particular damaged item or the supplier’s response, I’m not sure; the shared record doesn’t establish an incident or its resolution.

**Interviewer:** Where do those pieces of paper go after the receiving step?

**SME:** The receipt and signed packing slip go with the PO and supplier bill for accounting. Elena Corwin matches the bill, signed packing slip and PO before she enters the supplier bill in QuickBooks. She checks the item, quantities and price against the papers. Her accounting role is separate from Cora’s receiving work; Elena reports to Lydia Fenner, our controller, not to me.

**Interviewer:** What makes a packet ready to enter as a normal match?

**SME:** The relevant papers have to be there and the item, quantities and price have to match. If an item in the packet is missing or mismatched, it comes to me for exception approval before entry. An incomplete packet isn’t a normal match. A partial receipt can be legitimate, but the paper has to show what actually came in; it isn’t permission to pretend the full quantity arrived.

**Interviewer:** When you say an exception comes to you, can Jalen or Elena approve it if you’re unavailable?

**SME:** The procedure gives that exception approval to me. If I’m away, it comes back to me when I’m available; I wouldn’t assume Jalen, Elena or anyone else has that authority. I can tell you the control and handoff, but I’m not sure how long an individual packet would wait. The records don’t give a turnaround time.

**Interviewer:** About how much of this matching is there?

**SME:** Roughly 480 bill and receipt packets in a year. That includes partial deliveries and invoices. It isn’t 480 different purchase orders, and the figure doesn’t tell us how many packets were exceptions. Elena matches packets as bills and receipts come in.

## [12:00]

**Interviewer:** The records also show other axle prices. What prompted that comparison?

**SME:** We have two unsolicited written offers for SKU-0001. Brackenway Parts Resale LLC offered $240 each on October 16, and Willow Vale Trailer Supply LLC offered $250 each on October 19. Both are valid through November 15, 2026. Ridge Hollow remains the supplier of record at the snapshot. Neither alternative has been switched to or purchased from for this item.

**Interviewer:** What can you actually compare from those offers, and what would you want to avoid assuming?

**SME:** Both use a basis of 600 each annually in monthly releases of 50, with net-30 terms. Freight is included and sales tax is excluded, as in the current delivered-price comparison. The specification, packaged contents, lot size and stated lead-time basis match. But the lead-time duration isn’t specified. I’m not sure what that duration is, so I wouldn’t put a number on it. And the annual quantity in an offer is its comparison basis, not a commitment to buy.

**Interviewer:** How would you describe the price comparison without treating an offer as an invoice?

**SME:** At 600 axles, the selected current-supplier releases extend to $165,600 delivered, excluding sales tax. On the same quantity basis, the Brackenway offer extends to $144,000, and Willow Vale to $150,000. Those are separate quantity extensions. The alternative figures are not purchases or additional invoice totals, and they’re not evidence that a change has happened. I keep the written offers distinct from the POs.

**Interviewer:** Has someone formally tested the market for this axle before these offers?

**SME:** Ridge Hollow has supplied the comparable specification since June 2003, and supplier notices changed prices over time. No formal competitive re-quote was solicited for this SKU before the unsolicited 2026 alternatives. The sample tells us that much; it doesn’t let me reconstruct every supplier discussion over the years.

**Interviewer:** If an offer looks attractive, is the price enough for you to change suppliers?

**SME:** No. Engineering fit and compliance review and supplier and financial due diligence are still needed before a switch. The quoted specification and contents give us a basis for comparison, not a completed qualification. I’m not going to say those reviews are done when they aren’t established in the record.

**Interviewer:** Who needs to be involved if the floor asks about substituting material?

**SME:** Felix Harrow, our manufacturing engineer, and Iris Calder, our quality and warranty lead, must confirm fit and required specifications before a substitution. Brakes, tires, couplers, lighting and substitutions need fit and applicable compliance review. That’s distinct from my PO quantity approval or an accounting exception approval. Those approvals don’t by themselves establish that a different component fits a build.

## [16:00]

**Interviewer:** What if a material shortage affects the floor before all of that is settled?

**SME:** Theo Lowry or Avery Carver reports materials exceptions to Jonah Sutter for the schedule. That’s the floor-to-scheduling handoff described in the process. It doesn’t authorize using a substitute without Felix and Iris confirming the fit and required specifications. I wouldn’t infer that the whole plant stopped merely because a shortage affected some work.

**Interviewer:** From your position, where can an apparently straightforward question take a little untangling?

**SME:** “Do we have it?” is a good one. I need to know the SKU and the unit, and whether the question is about a physical count or an expected delivery. The count begins on paper and the working stock figure is in Excel; the purchase-order list is another record. If the question is about a supplier bill, Elena is looking at the bill and signed slip against the PO. There’s a handoff between those views; I can’t use a purchase order alone as proof of a receipt.

**Interviewer:** And what would you say about the difference between the axle receipt and that count you mentioned earlier?

**SME:** The October PO says 50 were received in full on October 15. The selected count says 21 were physically present on October 30, and the sheet agreed with that count. They answer different questions at different points in time. The sample doesn’t provide a complete movement ledger between those dates, so I’m not sure what individual uses or movements account for the difference. I wouldn’t invent them.

**Interviewer:** Are there measures you would need before describing the whole purchasing operation with confidence?

**SME:** I’d need more than these selected records. I’m not sure of the total inventory value, the number of exception packets, or the time taken to resolve them. I also can’t determine total company purchases from twelve selected axle lines. A blank or an absent measure isn’t a zero; it means the information isn’t provided here. The approximately 480 annual packets give a sense of the matching workload, but not an exception rate.

**Interviewer:** Before we finish, could you summarize the handoffs in the order they occur?

**SME:** Cora’s selected counts update the stock sheet; I consider on-hand stock, open POs and likely requirements. Jalen prepares a PO, I approve it and the quantity, then he numbers and emails it. Cora checks and records the actual delivery, tells Jalen first if it’s short or damaged, and updates the sheet. The receipt and signed slip accompany the PO and bill. Elena checks the packet before entry, and a missing or mismatched item comes to me for exception approval. [Pause.] That’s the normal sequence and the exception route, not a claim that every supplier delivery is identical.

**Interviewer:** That’s helpful. Is there anything you’d want a reader to remember about the examples we discussed?

**SME:** The October axle PO was fully received; the two later prices were offers only. The 21 axles were one selected count, not the whole inventory. And where the records stop, I’d rather say I’m not sure than turn an expected receipt or an attractive offer into something that already happened.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Devon. We’ll close there.
