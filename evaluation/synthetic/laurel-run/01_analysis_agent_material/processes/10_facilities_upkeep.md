# Facilities upkeep

Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc.  
Working procedure, fictional case, as of 2026-10-31

This covers the building, equipment service and ordinary office upkeep at the Hollow Glen factory. Simon Denby (E018), Facilities and IT Coordinator, keeps the maintenance list and coordinates service. Holly Darnell (E007), Reception and Admin Coordinator, handles office supplies and reception rounds. Owen Bellamy (E002), Plant Manager, owns the floor and is the immediate contact for urgent hazards reported by a supervisor.

## Routine service and requests

Simon tracks scheduled building and equipment service in Excel and coordinates with service providers. Use the maintenance list to see what service is scheduled and what still needs attention. Outlook is the route for routine maintenance requests. Give Simon enough detail to identify the place or equipment and the work needed; he can then coordinate the service provider and update his list as appropriate.

For example, if Holly finds an ordinary, nonurgent maintenance item on an office round, she can send the details to Simon in Outlook. He follows it up through the maintenance list and arranges service as needed. This is an example of the route, not a dated repair record. Office supplies are Holly's job to order; they don't need to be treated as an equipment-service request just because she notices them on a round.

Simon also maintains devices and keeps an Excel device and access list. He coordinates an outside IT support contractor as well as facilities service providers. Some small maintenance requests wait until he returns from IT work. As a rough workload guide, around 24 routine maintenance/IT handoffs in a year involve about 20 minutes of switching and coordination apiece. That is separate from Holly's rounds and from emergency response; it is not the total repair time.

## Urgent hazards are different

Don't leave an urgent hazard in the routine Outlook queue. Tell the supervisor immediately. The supervisor reports it immediately to Owen, and unsafe equipment is taken out of service. For instance, Theo Lowry (E019), Fabrication Supervisor, or Avery Carver (E020), Assembly Supervisor, would report an urgent hazard to Owen rather than wait for Simon to finish an IT job. Simon can coordinate service as needed, but scheduling service is not the immediate safety response.

Keep unsafe equipment out of service while the hazard is addressed, and follow applicable safety procedures before returning it to use. The maintenance list is a scheduling record, not permission to use unsafe equipment. The available records do not name a separate facilities backup or specify a release approval, so don't assume one. If Simon is unavailable, the urgent report still goes to the supervisor and Owen right away.

## Office rounds and records

Holly receives visitors and keeps reception supplies on hand. On her daily reception rounds she collects office wastebaskets. The ordinary rounds take about two hours a week across an approximately 50-week working year. Contract cleaners also serve the company; they are not Laurel Run employees. The records do not set out the cleaners' particular schedule or assign Holly's wastebasket collection to them.

Simon retains safety and building inspection records on paper. Keep those papers with the inspection records; the Excel maintenance list does not replace them. Service vendors and the outside IT contractor, like the contract cleaners, are not employees. No particular contractor price or inspection interval is specified here. For a routine request while Simon is away, use Outlook for his return; if it is an urgent hazard, use the immediate supervisor-to-Owen route above instead.
