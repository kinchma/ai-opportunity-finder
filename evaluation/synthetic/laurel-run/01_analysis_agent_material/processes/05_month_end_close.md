# Month-end close

**Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc. — fictional case, as of 2026-10-31**

We do this every month. Lydia Fenner (E004), Controller, owns the close and the paper checklist; Elena Corwin (E006), Accounting Clerk, handles the invoices and bills. The aim is to finish within five business days after month-end. That is a target, not a promise that every close is finished on that schedule. The reporting year here is 2025-11-01 through 2026-10-31, inclusive.

## Get the cutoff records together

1. Mateo Renshaw (E015), Shipping Coordinator, notes the shipment and sends shipping confirmation to Elena. Elena issues the dealer invoice in QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise at shipment and posts shipment invoices through the accounting cutoff. Under our fictional sales terms, shipment transfers control; an accepted order by itself is not revenue. Dealers approved for credit have net-30 terms from the shipment invoice. Taxes collected are excluded from revenue, and no freight was separately charged in this reporting year.

2. Elena posts received, approved supplier bills through the cutoff. In the purchasing and receiving routine, she matches each vendor bill to its PO and Cora Whitlock's signed packing slip before entering the bill in QuickBooks. There are roughly 480 bill/receipt packets a year, including partial deliveries and invoices; that is not necessarily 480 different POs. If a receipt or bill doesn't match, take it back to Jalen Sorrell (E013), Buyer, and Devon Kersey (E012), Materials and Purchasing Manager; Devon approves exceptions. This individual packet matching is separate from Lydia's close report work.

## Lydia's review

3. In QuickBooks, review the bank report and reconciliation, then the accounts receivable and accounts payable reports. Compare the shipping records with the dealer invoice records for the period. If something doesn't line up, go back to the underlying shipping or invoice record; ask Mateo or Elena about the missing detail as appropriate. Keep an unresolved item open for review rather than marking it complete.

4. Export selected reports to Excel and re-key the management summary from the reports. Lydia reviews and records approved adjustments as applicable. If an adjustment still needs approval or support, leave it open for Lydia to follow up; don't post it as approved. The comparison and reconciliation, selected report exports and summary re-keying are roughly four hours of work a month. That is not the whole close, and it doesn't include the individual bill packet matching or payroll entry.

5. Initial the paper checklist as the steps are completed. Lydia signs it after her review. Keep the checklist and the supporting accounting, shipping and report records for the period. If Lydia is away, leave her review and signoff pending; these records do not name an alternate signer.

## Two examples to keep straight

For the completed **September 2026** checklist, Elena posted shipment invoices through the September 30 cutoff and posted received approved bills on October 1. Lydia reviewed the bank reconciliation on October 2, AR and AP on October 5, and shipping against invoices and the management summary on October 6. On October 7 she reviewed adjustments, with none approved and pending, and signed the checklist.

For **October 2026**, the close was still open at the 2026-10-31 snapshot. October 31 was a Saturday; subsequent bank and close ledger work belongs on business days after month-end, and no later completion or approval is asserted here.

A shipment to check is Cedar Bend Trailer Sales (DLR-0001): ORD-2026-1120 and its separate WO-2026-1240 were for one Trailfield with a spare-tire mount only. Shipment and invoice INV-2026-1161 were dated October 27, for $4,920, with net-30 terms. This unit is included in October's 50 Trailfield shipments; the monthly log also shows 45 Enclosure and 30 Workspan shipments, 125 trailers in all. The log is a unit record, not a complete revenue ledger for options or replacement parts.
