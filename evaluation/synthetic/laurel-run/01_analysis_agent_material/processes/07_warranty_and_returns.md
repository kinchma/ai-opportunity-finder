# Warranty and returns

**Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc. — fictional procedure, as of 2026-10-31**

We work through our independent dealers, not direct retail sales. Priya Ellwood, Dealer Account Coordinator, takes the dealer's warranty message and passes it to Iris Calder, Quality and Warranty Lead. Iris follows the claim and approves the dealer repair before the work is done. There isn't one blanket coverage or return rule stated here; check the particulars of the claim rather than promising a repair, credit or refund at intake.

## When a dealer reports a problem

1. Ask the dealer to send the model, VIN, sale date, description of the problem and photos by email. Priya receives the message in Outlook, checks the dealer against the Excel dealer list and sends the details and photos to Iris. If something is missing, ask the dealer for it. Keep the correspondence with the case, including later replies.

2. Iris opens a claim with the next `CLM-YYYY-NNNN` identifier for that calendar year. This is its own sequence, not an order or work-order number. She uses the small Excel claim index and the original email thread to follow the case: dealer, model, reported date and status, with the problem details and photos in the thread. A VIN identifies the trailer; don't make one up from a work-order number. The sample claim file doesn't include VINs.

3. Iris checks the relevant component and the information from the dealer. She keeps the paper final-inspection checklist with the work-order packet, which can be referred to when relevant. Where a supplier may be involved, Iris discusses responsibility as applicable; that discussion is not itself a promise that the supplier will pay. If the facts or the proposed work aren't clear, get the needed details before treating the claim as ready for approval.

## Repair, return and close

Iris approves the dealer repair with the dealer **before work starts**. Keep the approval with the correspondence. If Iris is unavailable, the records do not name a backup repair approver: leave approval pending until she can review. Priya can pass dealer questions back to Iris rather than make a coverage decision herself.

A physical return needs authorization, identification and a disposition. Check those with Iris before the dealer sends the item; identify the item and the claim in the correspondence, and keep the authorization and disposition with the case. Not every claim necessarily involves a return. Don't assume a return has been accepted or disposed of just because it was requested.

For brakes, tires, couplers, lighting and substitutions, fit and applicable compliance review matter. Do not substitute material without Felix Harrow, Manufacturing Engineer, and Iris confirming fit and required specifications. The completed trailer's certification and load labels give its actual GVWR and payload; an axle rating by itself doesn't establish trailer payload. Don't promise a legal conclusion or say a unit is safe merely from a photo.

When the dealer sends evidence that the approved work is complete, Iris closes the claim and updates its status. Keep the completion evidence in the email thread. Until then, follow the open case in the index and correspondence; a blank date does not establish that approval was completed.
