# Service Ticket to Invoice

Owner: Dana Pruitt (Office Manager and Bookkeeper). Last updated August 2021.

## Purpose

Turn every completed service ticket into an invoice or a recorded payment in QuickBooks, and make sure nothing the tech did goes unbilled.

## The paper ticket

Techs write a three-part service ticket on every job: customer, address, equipment, work done, parts used (part number and quantity), hours, price, payment taken, and the customer's signature. White copy to the customer, yellow copy to the office, pink copy stays in the van book. About 75% of residential customers pay the tech on site by card (card reader on the tech's phone) or check. Commercial customers and property managers are invoiced.

## Steps

1. Techs drop yellow tickets in the tray at the office at the end of the day, or at the Monday morning meeting if they go home from the last job.
2. Dana sorts the tickets by date and tech and checks each against the dispatch spreadsheet (status Done).
3. Dana types each ticket into QuickBooks: customer, items, labor hours, parts from the price list, discount for Comfort Club members, payment received.
4. For paid tickets, Dana applies the card or check payment and matches it later on the bank deposit.
5. For commercial and property manager tickets, Dana creates the invoice and mails or emails it. Brookfield Pike Properties wants a PDF and the ticket number on every invoice.
6. File the yellow tickets by month in the file cabinet.

## Volumes and timing

About 5,200 service tickets and 290 commercial PM tickets a year, so about 22 tickets on a normal day and 35 or more in a heat wave. Dana figures about 4 minutes per ticket to type and check, longer when the writing is hard to read or a part number is missing. Commercial invoices go out on average about 8 days after the job.

## Example

Ticket SC-2026-4102, 2026-09-03, tech Kevin Amadi, Brookfield Pike Properties, building 7, replaced condenser fan motor, part FM-1/3HP-825, 1.5 hours. Yellow copy came in 2026-09-08 (Kevin went straight home Friday). Entered 2026-09-10 as INV-2026-1877, emailed 2026-09-11.

## Weekly checks

Every Friday Dana runs the QuickBooks open invoices report and compares it with the Done rows in the dispatch spreadsheet for the week. Any Done call with no invoice or payment gets a sticky note on the tech's mailbox.

## When things go wrong

- Missing yellow ticket: check the dispatch spreadsheet for Done calls with no ticket; ask the tech to bring the pink copy. Usually 25 to 35 tickets a month are late or missing at first.
- Part not on the price list: ask Lisa for the cost, ask Wes for the commercial markup.
- Customer disputes a charge: pull the yellow copy with the signature.
