# Purchasing and Inventory - Interview with Lisa Tran

Interviewee: Lisa Tran, Purchasing and Inventory Coordinator (since 2017)
Date: 2026-10-29
Fictional transcript. About 18 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] Role

**Interviewer:** Lisa, what does your job include?

**SME:** I do purchasing and the warehouse. I place the orders with Northgate and our other suppliers, I receive the deliveries, and I keep the parts cage in the warehouse stocked and counted. I report to Kyle. I've been here since 2017.

**Interviewer:** Does doing both buying and inventory help?

**SME:** Yes, a lot. When I count the cage I know what I already have on order, and when I order I know what's on the shelf. If those were two people, they'd be calling each other all day. It's about 60% purchasing and 40% the warehouse for me.

**Interviewer:** Who are your main suppliers?

**SME:** Northgate Supply, by far. All the Aerovance equipment, coils, motors, controls, most parts. About $1.2 million of equipment a year, plus the parts. Then Ridgeview HVAC Supply for filters and parts when Northgate is out, and Buckeye Sheet Metal for custom ductwork on installs.

## [02:30] Pricing and Northgate

**Interviewer:** How do you know you're getting good prices?

**SME:** We buy at our Northgate dealer price sheet. They send an updated sheet every January. Glen has bought from Northgate since he started in 1994. As far as I know nobody has asked anyone else to quote equipment since we became an Aerovance dealer, which was before my time. Northgate was the only Aerovance distributor around here until recently.

**Interviewer:** Until recently?

**SME:** Ridgeview became an Aerovance distributor in 2025. In March their rep came by and left a written quote for the same Aerovance models we buy, at 7.5% below our Northgate prices. Same units, same model numbers. It's in the purchasing binder, and I gave a copy to Kyle.

**Interviewer:** What happened with it?

**SME:** Kyle has it to review. He said he'd talk to Glen. Glen is loyal to Northgate, they've been good to us, they take care of warranty returns, and the counter guys know Ray. I don't know if anyone has asked Northgate to match it. I haven't heard anything since March.

**Interviewer:** Do you know what 7.5% would mean on your volume?

**SME:** On $1.2 million of equipment, it's about $90,000 a year. I did that math when the quote came in. I'm not sure how much of that we'd really see, it depends on whether Northgate matches and what Ridgeview's delivery is like. But it's a big number for us.

## [06:00] Purchase orders and approvals

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a purchase order.

**SME:** I write the PO in the paper PO book, PO-2026 and a number, with the supplier, items, quantities, prices, and the job number if it's for a job. Anything over $500 needs Kyle's signature before I place it. Kyle's out in the field a lot, so I text him a picture of the PO, and when he texts back OK, I order. I get the signature later.

**Interviewer:** How often are POs over $500?

**SME:** Every equipment order, basically. Six condensers and coils is $20,000. A single furnace is over $500. So almost every install order goes through Kyle. Parts orders are usually under.

**Interviewer:** How long does it take Kyle to answer?

**SME:** It depends where he is. If he's in the office, five minutes. If he's on a roof or with a customer, hours. Like the April order in the procedure, PO-2026-0412, six condensers and coils for JOB-2026-0301 to 0306, $19,860. I texted him at 9:15 and got the OK at 1:40, ordered at 1:52. That was close to the 2 pm cutoff. If he answers after 2, the order goes the next day and the delivery is a day later.

**Interviewer:** Does that happen often?

**SME:** A few times a month. Usually it doesn't matter, but when it's for an install Ben has scheduled, it matters. Then Ben moves the install or Ray goes to the counter for it.

**Interviewer:** Has anyone talked about raising the $500 limit?

**SME:** Kyle set it in 2019 when he became operations manager. It hasn't changed. Equipment prices went up a lot since then. I'm not sure who would decide that, probably Kyle and Glen.

## [10:00] Deliveries and receiving

**Interviewer:** How do deliveries work?

**SME:** Northgate delivers free the next morning if I order online or by phone by 2 pm. I'm at the warehouse door when the truck comes, I check everything against the PO, and I file the packing slip with the PO for Dana. Wrong parts I call in and they swap them on the next delivery.

**Interviewer:** And install equipment?

**SME:** For installs Ray picks it up at the Northgate counter in the morning. That's been the way since before me. I order it the same way, I just mark it "will call" instead of delivery. I could have it delivered to our warehouse the morning before, the delivery is the same price, free. Nobody has asked me to change it.

## [11:00] Other suppliers, returns and bills

**Interviewer:** How do you use Ridgeview today?

**SME:** Filters, mostly, and parts when Northgate is out of something. Their filter prices are good. They deliver twice a week. That's about $40,000 a year with them. We've never bought equipment from them.

**Interviewer:** What about warranty parts and returns?

**SME:** When a part fails under warranty, Ben files the claim on the Aerovance portal and the failed part goes back to Northgate with a tag. Northgate gives us a credit on the statement. I keep a list of open returns on a sheet taped inside the cage door. Sometimes the credits take two or three months to show up and Dana asks me about them at month-end.

**Interviewer:** How do bills get to Dana?

**SME:** I staple the packing slip to my copy of the PO and put it in Dana's tray. She enters the bill in QuickBooks when the Northgate statement comes and matches it to the POs. If a PO doesn't have a packing slip, she comes and asks me. That's a few a month, usually a counter pickup by Ray where the slip stayed in his truck.

## [12:30] The cage and van stock

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the parts cage.

**SME:** It's the caged area in the warehouse with the common parts: capacitors, contactors, motors, igniters, filters, refrigerant. I count it the last Friday of every month into the Cage Count spreadsheet and reorder anything below the minimum. The count takes me most of that afternoon.

**Interviewer:** And the vans?

**SME:** Each service van carries a standard stock, about 120 common parts. On Monday mornings the techs restock from the cage and write what they took on the clipboard by the cage door. Some write it all, some write some of it. I don't count the vans. Nobody does. I can't tell you what's on any van right now.

**Interviewer:** What happens when a tech needs a part he doesn't have?

**SME:** He drives to the cage, or to the Northgate counter if we don't have it. The techs say that's about two times a week each. Some of them more. With 14 techs that's a lot of trips. Each one is probably 45 minutes to an hour by the time he gets back to the customer. Trevor hates it because his afternoon moves.

**Interviewer:** Why does a van run out of something it's supposed to carry?

**SME:** Because the restock depends on the clipboard and the tech's memory. If a tech used three capacitors on Thursday and forgot one on Monday, he's short the next week. I'd guess most of those trips are for parts that should have been on the van.

## [15:30] Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** How do you decide the minimums in the cage?

**SME:** From experience, mostly. I set them when I started and I raise one when we run out twice. In a heat wave capacitors and contactors go fast, so I double those in June. There's no report behind it, it's just me watching the shelves.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing?

**SME:** Know what's on the vans. And get an answer on the Ridgeview quote, one way or the other. Those are the two.

**Interviewer:** Anything else I should know?

**SME:** Only that when I'm out, purchasing mostly waits. Kyle can place an order, he has the Northgate login. The cage count just doesn't happen that month.

**Interviewer:** Thanks, Lisa.
