# Payroll and Timesheets - Interview with Ruth Vossberg

Interviewee: Ruth Vossberg, Vice President and co-owner (since 1994)
Date: 2026-10-30
Fictional transcript. About 18 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] Role

**Interviewer:** Ruth, you started the company with Glen. What do you do today?

**SME:** Payroll and HR, mostly. I run payroll every other Friday for all 35 people in ADP RUN. I do the hiring paperwork, benefits enrollment, the personnel files, PTO. I review Dana's month-end checklist. In the early days I did all the books and the phones too, so I know QuickBooks, but Dana has that now.

**Interviewer:** How do payroll and HR fit together?

**SME:** It's the same files. When someone is hired, I set them up in ADP from the new-hire packet I already collected for the personnel file. When a raise or an apprentice step changes, I see it in the file and change it in ADP. It would be silly to have two people doing that. In the role matrix I'm about 55% payroll, 30% HR and the rest is general management with Glen.

**Interviewer:** How big is the field crew now?

**SME:** Twenty-four in the field. Fourteen service techs, eight installers and the two apprentices. Plus eleven of us in the office, including Glen, Kyle and me.

## [02:30] The timesheets

**Interviewer:** How does time get recorded?

**SME:** The field staff fill in a paper weekly timesheet. Every day: start time, lunch, end time, the job numbers they worked, on-call hours and overtime. They sign it. Service techs give theirs to Sam, installers give theirs to Ben. Sam and Ben initial them and put them in my tray on Monday morning. The office staff use the sign-in sheet at the front desk, and salaried people only record time off.

**Interviewer:** Why paper?

**SME:** Because it's what we've always done, and the guys are used to it. When Kyle bought Housecall Pro, I think it had a time clock in it, but the techs never used the app, so we never got that far. I'm not sure what it does for time.

**Interviewer:** How does on-call work for pay?

**SME:** One tech is on call each week. He gets $150 for the week, plus overtime for the hours he actually works after hours. He writes those on the timesheet. I compare his on-call hours with the after-hours calls in the dispatch spreadsheet, because sometimes the hours are written on the wrong day.

## [05:30] Running payroll

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a payroll.

**SME:** The Monday after the pay period ends, I collect two weeks of field timesheets. There are usually three or four missing, so I chase those, I call Sam or Ben or the tech. Then I check every sheet. I add up the daily hours, check lunch, check overtime over 40 in the week, and compare the on-call hours.

**Interviewer:** Then?

**SME:** Then I type each person's hours into ADP RUN: regular, overtime, on-call and PTO. Twenty-four field people, two weeks each. Then I fix the questions, times I can't read, missing end times, a weekend call that didn't get written down. There are about six corrections a payroll. Then I preview the payroll in ADP, compare it with last period, and submit it by Tuesday at 5 pm. The sheets go in the binder for the year.

**Interviewer:** How long does that take?

**SME:** About five hours a payroll, from collecting the sheets to submitting. More in January and July when there's a lot of overtime and on-call. Twenty-six payrolls a year, so it's a good chunk of my year.

**Interviewer:** Which part takes longest?

**SME:** The checking and the typing. Adding up the days by hand and then putting them into ADP person by person. If the sheets were right and already added up, the ADP part would be fast.

## [09:00] An example

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example of a correction?

**SME:** The pay period ending October 17th, PAY-2026-21. Field hours were 1,912 regular and 188 overtime. Frank Delgado wrote 52 hours on his week, but when I added up his days it came to 49.5. I checked with Sam, who knew his jobs, and Sam confirmed 49.5. We paid 49.5. I submitted at 3:40 on Tuesday the 20th.

**Interviewer:** Does that kind of thing happen every payroll?

**SME:** Something like it, yes. Usually it's the addition, or the lunch, or the end time written as 5 when the dispatch sheet shows the last call ended at 6:30. Most of the time it's an honest mistake, the guys fill them in on Friday from memory.

**Interviewer:** Do the job numbers on the timesheet get used for anything?

**SME:** Kyle wanted them to see hours per job. I don't do anything with them for payroll. I'm not sure Kyle has had time to use them either.

## [12:00] Overtime, PTO and rules

**Interviewer:** How is overtime approved?

**SME:** Kyle approves it, often verbally on the phone, and then he initials the sheet. If I see overtime with no initials, I ask Kyle. Overtime is time and a half over 40 hours in the week, Sunday to Saturday. Holidays don't count toward the 40.

**Interviewer:** And PTO?

**SME:** Paper slips approved by Sam, Ben or Kyle. I keep the balances in a small Excel sheet because the PTO balances in ADP RUN were never set up when we started with ADP. So I update my sheet and type the PTO hours into ADP. People ask me their balance and I look it up in my sheet.

**Interviewer:** What about the apprentices?

**SME:** They're paid hourly at their step rate. Their step changes on their anniversary date. I have the dates in the personnel files and I watch for them. Uma's anniversary is in the spring, Victor's next year.

## [13:30] New hires and benefits

**Interviewer:** How does a new hire get set up?

**SME:** Kyle or Ben does the interviewing and decides. Then I put together the new-hire packet: the application, the I-9 and W-4, direct deposit form, the handbook signature page, a copy of the driver's license for the van insurance. That goes in the paper personnel file. I enter them in ADP RUN from the packet. It's about an hour per new hire. We hired four people this year, including Victor.

**Interviewer:** And benefits?

**SME:** Health insurance through a broker, enrollment in November for January. I collect the paper enrollment forms and send them to the broker, then set up the deductions in ADP. The 401(k) deductions are in ADP too. Open enrollment is a busy two weeks for me because it's on top of payroll.

**Interviewer:** Do employees ask you questions about their pay?

**SME:** All the time. Why is my check different, how much PTO do I have, when is my step raise. I answer from ADP and my PTO sheet. A few a week. If they could look up their PTO themselves that would save some of it, but our ADP isn't set up for that, because the balances aren't in there.

**Interviewer:** How do you keep track of driver's license renewals and certifications?

**SME:** The license copies are in the personnel files with the expiration date written on the folder tab. The techs' refrigerant certifications Kyle keeps. I look at the folder tabs every January. I'm not sure there's a better way, it's only 24 people.

## [15:00] When things go wrong

**Interviewer:** What happens if a timesheet is missing on Tuesday?

**SME:** We pay 40 hours and correct it on the next payroll. That happens maybe once a month. The guy is usually unhappy if he had overtime, so we try not to.

**Interviewer:** And if you're out?

**SME:** Payroll is late unless I do it from home, which I've done. There's no named backup. Dana has seen me do it, and she has a login to look at reports, but she's never run one on her own. I'd like to fix that, honestly. Glen and I aren't getting younger.

**Interviewer:** Does Glen get involved in payroll?

**SME:** He looks at the payroll total compared to revenue in Dana's monthly report. He doesn't touch ADP.

## [17:00] Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing about payroll?

**SME:** Get the hours in already added up and checked, so I'm not doing arithmetic on 24 sheets every two weeks. And maybe get someone else trained so it's not just me.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**SME:** The guys are good about it mostly. It's just a lot of paper.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Ruth.
