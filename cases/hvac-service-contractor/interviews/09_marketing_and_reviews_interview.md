# Marketing and Reviews - Interview with Jolene Fairweather

Interviewee: Jolene Fairweather, Customer Service Rep and Reception (since 2021)
Date: 2026-10-30
Fictional transcript. About 19 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] Role

**Interviewer:** Jolene, tell me about your job.

**SME:** I'm a CSR and the receptionist. I answer the phones with Marisol, I greet anyone who comes in the front door, and I run the Comfort Club tune-up booking. I also do the Facebook page and answer the Google reviews, when I have time. I've been here since 2021. I report to Kyle.

**Interviewer:** How much of your time goes to marketing?

**SME:** Very little. Maybe 5% in the role matrix. It's whatever I can fit in between calls. Nobody here has marketing as their job. Glen decides the radio spots and the postcards, and the budget, about $38,000 a year. Most of that is radio and postcards.

**Interviewer:** What does the company do for marketing?

**SME:** Repeat customers and referrals are the big thing. About 70% of our residential customers have used us before. Then the website, the Google Business Profile, the Facebook page, the Comfort Club postcards in February and August, radio in January and July, and the truck wraps. That's it. No Instagram, no LinkedIn, no TikTok. Kyle talked about Instagram once.

## [02:30] Google reviews

**Interviewer:** Let's start with reviews. Where do they stand?

**SME:** We're at 4.3 stars on Google with 112 reviews, as of this week. I check it now and then, maybe once a week if I remember. When I see a new review I answer it, thank them if it's good, and if it's bad I tell Kyle and answer something polite.

**Interviewer:** How do customers end up leaving reviews?

**SME:** Mostly on their own. We don't ask in any regular way. A few techs ask happy customers, Sam does, Derek does. Most don't. There's no card or text or anything after a job. When somebody's really unhappy they find us on their own, so the bad ones come in whether we ask or not.

**Interviewer:** Do you know how many reviews came in this year?

**SME:** I'm not sure exactly. Maybe two or three a month. It's in the activity sheet I keep, the marketing one, roughly. Compared to how many jobs we do, it's not many. We do about 5,200 service calls and 310 installs a year.

**Interviewer:** What were the bad reviews about?

**SME:** The ones I remember, waiting for a callback, and one about a tech showing up later than the window. A couple about price. The good ones usually name the tech.

## [06:00] Facebook

**Interviewer:** And the Facebook page?

**SME:** About 1,900 followers. I post when I have time. This year it was nine posts. Things like a picture of a new truck, a reminder about furnace tune-ups, a holiday hours post, Sam's 20 years. Glen likes it when I post the crew pictures.

**Interviewer:** Do you track how the posts do?

**SME:** Not really. I look at the likes when I remember. Nobody's asked me for numbers. The activity sheet has the dates and what I posted, and sometimes the likes.

**Interviewer:** Do people message you on Facebook?

**SME:** Now and then someone sends a message asking for service. I see it when I go on the page, so it might be a day or two. Then I book it like a phone call.

## [08:30] Website and measuring

**Interviewer:** What about the website?

**SME:** It was built in 2019 by a local designer. Glen gets it updated about once a year, prices and pictures. The request form goes to service@, which is the same inbox the answering service uses. Dana counts the web requests at month-end, about 410 this year.

**Interviewer:** Do you know where new customers come from?

**SME:** Not for service calls. We don't ask, or if we ask it's not written anywhere. Nadia asks on install proposals and writes it on the folder. I'm not sure anyone adds those up. So I couldn't tell you if the radio works better than the postcards.

**Interviewer:** Is there any follow-up after a job?

**SME:** Not from the office. The Housecall Pro text goes out the night before the appointment, that's the only text a customer gets from us. After the job, nothing.

## [10:30] Comfort Club booking

**Interviewer:** Tell me about booking the Comfort Club tune-ups.

**SME:** That's the biggest thing I do besides the phones. We have 640 members, two tune-ups each, spring and fall, so 1,280 tune-ups a year. In March and September I print the member list from the Comfort Club tab, and Marisol and I call every member to book. If nobody answers, I leave a message and try again later in the week.

**Interviewer:** How many calls does it take?

**SME:** Usually two or three calls to book one member. Some people answer the first time, some never call back. Each call is a few minutes, about four minutes with the message or the booking. So it's a lot of hours in March and September, on top of the regular phones.

**Interviewer:** What happens to members you can't reach?

**SME:** The procedure says if we don't reach them by the end of the season, they're skipped until the next season. This year, about 236 tune-ups never got booked out of the 1,280. Some of those people call later upset that they paid for two tune-ups and only got one. Then we squeeze them in.

**Interviewer:** Do members renew anyway?

**SME:** It renews on their card automatically, $189. Some cancel when they call about a missed tune-up. Dana gives me the list of cards that failed and I call those too. I'm not sure how many we lose each year, Dana would know from the member count.

**Interviewer:** Is there an example?

**SME:** Linda Garvey, member CC-0418. Fall tune-up booked on the third call, September 22nd, done October 6th. That's a normal one. I write the attempts in the notes column in the Comfort Club tab so Marisol knows who I already called.

## [13:00] Radio, postcards and the trucks

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the radio and the postcards.

**SME:** Radio is January and July, about $4,500 a campaign. Glen picks the station and does the voice himself, people recognize him. The postcards go to every Comfort Club member in February and August, about $1,100 each mailing, with a reminder that tune-up season is coming and a coupon for a friend. I'm not sure how many coupons come back, I think Dana has seen a few.

**Interviewer:** Do calls go up after the radio?

**SME:** It feels like it, but January and July are our busiest months anyway because of the weather, so I couldn't tell you what's the radio and what's the cold. We've never compared.

**Interviewer:** And the trucks?

**SME:** All 19 vehicles are wrapped, the 16 vans and the 3 install trucks. People call and say "I saw your truck in my neighbor's driveway." I hear that a lot. That's probably our best advertising, honestly, along with word of mouth.

## [14:00] Front desk

**Interviewer:** What else lands on the front desk?

**SME:** Walk-ins paying a bill, a vendor dropping off, a delivery that should have gone to the warehouse door. Applicants dropping off a resume, I give those to Ruth. And the mail. When Marisol is on dispatch, I'm the only one on the phones and the front door, so marketing doesn't happen those days at all.

**Interviewer:** Who answers the Google questions, like hours or whether you service a certain brand?

**SME:** Those come in on the profile and I answer when I check it. A couple of times somebody asked a question and I didn't see it for a week.

## [15:30] Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing in marketing?

**SME:** Ask every happy customer for a review, the same way every time. And post on Facebook regularly instead of when I remember. Those are small things, but nobody owns them.

**Interviewer:** And in Comfort Club booking?

**SME:** Fewer calls. Most members just want to pick a time. Calling 640 people two or three times twice a year is a lot.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**SME:** Just that the front desk is busy. When someone walks in, the phone still rings. Marketing is always the thing that waits.

**Interviewer:** Thanks, Jolene.
