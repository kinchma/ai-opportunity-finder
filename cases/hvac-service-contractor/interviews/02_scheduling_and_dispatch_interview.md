# Scheduling and Dispatch - Interview with Trevor Haskins

Interviewee: Trevor Haskins, Dispatcher (since 2016)
Date: 2026-10-27
Fictional transcript. About 17 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] Role

**Interviewer:** Trevor, tell me about your role.

**SME:** I'm the dispatcher. I build every tech's day and keep it running. Service calls, the Comfort Club tune-ups, the commercial PM visits Wes gives me. I've done it since 2016. Before that I was a CSR here for a year.

**Interviewer:** How many techs are you dispatching?

**SME:** Fourteen service techs, plus the two apprentices ride with Sam, so I don't schedule them separately. The install crews are Ben's, I don't touch them. I just need to know if Ben borrows a tech.

**Interviewer:** What does the day look like in numbers?

**SME:** Normal day is 20 to 30 service calls, more in a heat wave. In spring and fall add the tune-ups. One or two commercial PM visits most days. The day I always point to is July 14th this year, 31 calls on the board.

## [02:30] Building the day

**Interviewer:** Walk me through how you build the day.

**SME:** I'm in by seven. First I look at the spreadsheet, Dispatch 2026, for new rows from yesterday, and Marisol hands me the after-hours ones when she's typed them in. Then I go to the whiteboard. Techs across the top, two-hour windows down the side, a magnet with the job number for every call. Red magnets for P1, no heat or no cool. P1s first, then booked calls, then tune-ups and PM visits.

**Interviewer:** How do you decide who gets what?

**SME:** Skill and where they are. Commercial work goes to Sam, Derek or Hector, they're the ones who know rooftop units. Newer guys get tune-ups and simpler calls. I try to keep guys in one area, but honestly it's from memory, I know where everybody lives and which towns are close. There's no map or anything.

**Interviewer:** Then what?

**SME:** Then I copy the tech name and the window from the board back into the spreadsheet row, so the spreadsheet matches. I text each tech their first two jobs. The rest I give them by phone when they call in done.

**Interviewer:** So the board and the spreadsheet both hold the schedule.

**SME:** Right. The board is the real one. The procedure says if they don't match, the board wins, and I fix the spreadsheet at the end of the day. That's usually 15, 20 minutes at four o'clock, going row by row. On a crazy day it doesn't get done until the next morning.

## [06:00] During the day

**Interviewer:** What happens when a tech finishes a job?

**SME:** He calls me. "Done at Smith, what's next." I mark it Done on the spreadsheet, wipe the magnet, give him the next job and the address. Fourteen guys, four or five jobs each, plus guys calling with questions or parts, that's 60 to 80 calls from techs a day. On top of the customer calls that come to me when Marisol and Jolene are busy.

**Interviewer:** What about customers asking where the tech is?

**SME:** That's the CSRs mostly. They come back and look at the board. If the tech hasn't called in, they ask me, and I call the tech. In summer that's a lot of calls, 30 or 40 a day. Customers want a time, and all I have is a two-hour window and whatever the last guy told me.

**Interviewer:** And when the day changes?

**SME:** Every day changes. Somebody runs long, somebody needs a part, a P1 comes in at noon. Take July 14th: Ivan was on a compressor replacement that ran way long. I moved his two afternoon calls to Grant, moved the magnets, updated the two rows and called both customers. One of them had already gotten the Housecall Pro text the night before with Ivan's name, so she called asking who Grant was.

## [09:30] Housecall Pro

**Interviewer:** Tell me about Housecall Pro.

**SME:** Kyle bought it in March 2024. The idea was the techs would get their jobs on the phone and do the tickets in the app, and I'd drag and drop instead of the board. We tried it for a few weeks. The older techs hated the tickets on the phone, and I couldn't see the whole day the way I can on the board. So it went back to the board.

**Interviewer:** What is it used for now?

**SME:** Reminder texts. Late afternoon Kyle copies the next day's calls from the spreadsheet into Housecall Pro so the customers get a text the night before. That's it. I think it's about $299 a month. Kyle does it himself because he's the one who knows it, maybe 20 minutes a night. If he's out, sometimes the texts don't go.

**Interviewer:** So the schedule lives in three places at the end of the day.

**SME:** Board, spreadsheet, and the next day in Housecall Pro. Yeah. When I say it like that it sounds bad. It works because I'm here.

## [11:00] Tune-ups and commercial visits

**Interviewer:** How do Comfort Club tune-ups and commercial PM visits get onto the board?

**SME:** Tune-ups come to me as rows in the spreadsheet once Jolene or Marisol book them with the member. In March and April and then September and October, that's a big chunk of the day, sometimes eight or ten tune-ups. I give those to the newer techs and I try to stack them in one town so they're not driving all over. Members get priority scheduling, so if a member calls with a repair it goes ahead of a non-member.

**Interviewer:** And commercial?

**SME:** Wes gives me a list at the start of the month of which agreements have visits due. I book those with the building contacts, mostly property managers. Brookfield Pike has 14 buildings, so their visits I do in blocks, two or three buildings a day for Sam or Hector. If an agreement isn't on Wes's list, I don't book it. I just go by his list.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever had a commercial customer call about a visit that wasn't booked?

**SME:** A few times this year. The customer says "you were supposed to be here in May" and I look and they're not on the list. Then I hand it to Wes, and usually it turns out the agreement ran out. That's his side, I'm not sure how he tracks the dates.

## [12:45] P1 calls and on-call

**Interviewer:** How do P1 calls work during the day?

**SME:** A P1 bumps something. I look at who is closest and who's on something short, call him, and move his next call to someone else or to later. I call the customer whose call got moved. On a cold Monday in January I might have ten P1s before noon, and the whole board is red. That's when I don't touch the spreadsheet until the afternoon.

**Interviewer:** And at night?

**SME:** One tech is on call each week. Midstate texts him directly for no heat and no cool. He goes, writes a ticket, and the next morning Marisol and I match it up with the message. I don't dispatch at night. Nobody does, really, it's between Midstate and the on-call guy.

## [14:30] Backup and risks

**Interviewer:** What happens when you're out?

**SME:** Marisol runs it. She's good, she knows the customers because she booked most of them. The problem is then Jolene is alone on the phones. A week of vacation for me in the summer is rough for those two.

**Interviewer:** Has the board ever been lost?

**SME:** Once somebody's elbow wiped half of Tuesday. I rebuilt it from the spreadsheet and the texts I'd sent. Took about an hour.

**Interviewer:** How do you keep track of calls that need a second visit?

**SME:** Status Needs Return or Parts on Order in the spreadsheet. When Lisa tells me the part is in, I look for those rows and call the customer to rebook. If I miss the message from Lisa, the row just sits. Every couple of weeks I filter for those statuses and go through them. Last time I found four that were waiting on parts that had already come in.

## [16:00] Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing?

**SME:** I'd like to see where the guys are without calling them. And not keep the board and the spreadsheet both. But I don't want something where I can't see the whole day at once. That's why Housecall didn't work for me.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**SME:** Parts. When a tech doesn't have a part on the van he's gone for an hour, and my whole afternoon moves. Lisa can tell you more about that. That's probably my biggest headache after the phones.

**Interviewer:** Thanks, Trevor.
