# Month-End Close - Interview with Dana Pruitt

Interviewee: Dana Pruitt, Office Manager and Bookkeeper (since 2008)
Date: 2026-10-30
Fictional transcript. About 18 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] What month-end means here

**Interviewer:** Dana, thanks for a second interview. What does month-end close mean at Larkmoor?

**SME:** Two things. Closing the month in QuickBooks, so all the invoices, bills, payments, the bank and the credit card are in and reconciled. And then the Monthly Numbers workbook, which is the report Glen, Ruth and Kyle look at. Ruth reviews my checklist and initials it, and I print the workbook for Glen.

**Interviewer:** What is the target?

**SME:** The report on Glen's desk by the 10th business day of the next month. Lately it's been closer to the 12th. September's was printed October 16th, that was the 12th business day.

**Interviewer:** What holds it up?

**SME:** The last yellow tickets, mostly. I can't close a month until the tickets are in, and in September the last ones came in on October 6th. Then the bank reconciliations, then the workbook. The workbook is the part that takes the longest of the things I control.

## [03:00] The checklist

**Interviewer:** Walk me through the checklist.

**SME:** It's on paper, and there's a copy in the data folder. One, all yellow tickets entered. Two, all install folders that Ben marked Done are invoiced. Three, all supplier bills entered from the PO packing slips and matched to the Northgate statement. Four, reconcile the two bank accounts and the credit card. Five, record the Comfort Club renewals that were charged in the month, from the card processor report. Six, record the payroll journal from the ADP RUN reports. Seven, run the P&L and AR aging. Eight, build the workbook. Nine, Ruth reviews.

**Interviewer:** How long do steps one through seven take?

**SME:** About 12 to 15 hours in a month, spread over the first week or so. That's normal bookkeeping. I don't mind that part.

**Interviewer:** And step eight?

**SME:** About 10 hours a month. That's the copying and counting.

## [05:30] Building the Monthly Numbers workbook

**Interviewer:** Tell me what goes into the workbook.

**SME:** Calls per tech and calls by type, I copy from the dispatch spreadsheet and count the rows with status Done for the month. Installs and install revenue, I copy from Ben's install log. Commercial agreements active, renewed and lost, from Wes's agreement sheet. Comfort Club member count, from the Comfort Club tab. Revenue by type, I re-type the totals from the QuickBooks P&L. And the web form count, I count the emails in service@.

**Interviewer:** Why does it take 10 hours?

**SME:** Because the three spreadsheets are all laid out differently and none of them were built for this. The dispatch spreadsheet has duplicate rows, from people who called and emailed, so I have to take those out before I count. Trevor's status column isn't always updated by month-end. Ben's install log has the install date but some rows have the sale date in the same column. Wes's sheet has notes in the cells where I want numbers.

**Interviewer:** So you clean each one before you can use it.

**SME:** Every month. I copy the month's rows into a scratch tab, remove duplicates, fix what I can, then count with formulas. Then I check the counts against QuickBooks. If the install count in Ben's log doesn't match the install invoices in QuickBooks, I go through the folders with Ben. That's a couple of hours by itself some months.

**Interviewer:** What did September look like?

**SME:** 512 service calls, 31 installs, 2 commercial agreements lost. The install count took me two tries because two jobs were in the log with the sale date in August.

## [09:30] Who uses the report

**Interviewer:** Who reads the workbook?

**SME:** Glen looks at revenue by type and the install count. Kyle looks at calls per tech and callbacks. Ruth looks at AR aging and payroll as a percent of revenue. Wes gets the commercial page by email. That's it.

**Interviewer:** Has anyone asked for something different?

**SME:** Kyle asked for a weekly version in the summer, calls per tech and callbacks every Monday. There wasn't time to build it. If the monthly takes 10 hours, a weekly would be half my week.

**Interviewer:** Is the workbook the same every month?

**SME:** Same layout since 2021, I copy last month's file and change the month. The formulas are mine. If I'm out, Ruth could print it if it was done, but she wouldn't build it.

## [11:30] Bills and the Northgate statement

**Interviewer:** How does the supplier side work at month-end?

**SME:** Lisa puts the packing slip with the PO in my tray. When the Northgate statement comes, I enter the bills and match them to the POs. Northgate is most of our purchases, so it's most of the work. If a PO has no packing slip, usually it's a counter pickup and the slip is in Ray's truck, I ask Lisa. Warranty credits show up on the statement two or three months later and I match those to Lisa's returns list.

**Interviewer:** Do statements usually match?

**SME:** Most months there are three or four lines I have to call Northgate about. They're helpful. It's an hour or so.

## [13:00] Comfort Club and payroll entries

**Interviewer:** How do you record the Comfort Club renewals?

**SME:** Members renew automatically on their card every year. The card processor gives me a monthly report of the renewals charged, and I enter them in QuickBooks as one deposit with a list. Any card that failed, I give the list to Jolene to call. A handful a month.

**Interviewer:** And payroll?

**SME:** Ruth runs payroll in ADP RUN. At month-end I take the ADP reports for the two or three payrolls in the month and enter one journal entry for wages, taxes and benefits. That's quick, maybe 30 minutes.

## [14:00] Receivables

**Interviewer:** What do you do with the AR aging report?

**SME:** I run it at month-end and give it to Ruth. Anything over 60 days, I call. It's mostly commercial and property managers, plus some residential customers who were invoiced instead of paying the tech. Most of the late ones are late because our invoice went out late, honestly. Brookfield pays 30 days from the invoice date, so if I invoice eight days after the job, that's 38 days from the work.

**Interviewer:** How big is the over-60 list usually?

**SME:** Ten to fifteen invoices most months. I call each one and write a note on the report. Ruth looks at it and asks me about the big ones. If something is over 90 days, Glen decides whether to send it to collections. That's two or three a year.

**Interviewer:** Is the install balance handled the same way?

**SME:** The install balance is due at completion. Nadia collects the deposit, and the crew lead or Ben collects the balance at the walk-through, or the financing company pays us. Financing payments come in about a week later and I match them to the job.

## [15:00] When things go wrong

**Interviewer:** What happens when you're out at month-end?

**SME:** The close waits. Ruth can enter bills. She doesn't do the bank reconciliations. In March I had the flu for four days at the start of the month and February's report went out on the 15th business day.

**Interviewer:** Is anyone learning your part?

**SME:** Not right now. Ruth knows QuickBooks. Nobody else knows the workbook.

**Interviewer:** You also mentioned in the last interview that you handle IT. Does that hit month-end?

**SME:** It does when something breaks. The printer always seems to jam the day I'm printing the workbook. The August phone outage was in the middle of the July close and pushed it two days.

## [17:00] Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing about month-end?

**SME:** Not building the workbook by hand. The bookkeeping I'll always do. The copying from three spreadsheets and re-typing from QuickBooks into Excel, that's 10 hours a month that doesn't need to be me. That's 120 hours a year.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**SME:** Just that the numbers are only as good as the spreadsheets. If Trevor's statuses are behind, my call count is behind.

**Interviewer:** Thanks again, Dana.
