Trailer Care & Checklist

Fictional demonstration: Laurel Run Trailer Works, its people, dealers, and products are invented. No purchases or messages are processed.

A simple pre-trip check

Use this general checklist as a conversation starter, not as a complete inspection or a substitute for the finished trailer's manual, certification labels, tow-vehicle instructions, or local requirements. Print this page using your browser if a paper copy is useful.

  1. Read the labels. Confirm the trailer's ratings and the limits of the tow vehicle, hitch, and other components. Do not assume payload from an axle rating or deck size.
  2. Plan and secure the load. Check weight and distribution, secure items with appropriate equipment, and follow applicable cargo-securement rules. Close and secure gates, doors, and ramps.
  3. Check the connection. Follow the instructions for the coupler, safety chains, electrical connection, and any breakaway system fitted to the trailer.
  4. Inspect tires and wheels. Look for visible damage and check tire pressure and wheel-fastener requirements against the relevant labels and instructions.
  5. Test lights and brakes. Confirm the lights and any trailer brakes operate properly before setting out. Address faults before travel.
  6. Look over the trailer. Check the deck, frame, doors, ramps, and attachment points for damage or loose parts. Have concerns inspected by a qualified professional.

After the trip

Remove debris, note damage, and arrange service as directed by the trailer and component instructions. Maintenance needs vary with use, equipment, and conditions; use the intervals and procedures supplied for the specific unit.
