# Onboarding

**Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc. — working procedure as of 2026-10-31**  
This is a fictional academic case. Nia Hadley, Office and Payroll Administrator, keeps the onboarding paperwork and also prepares payroll. The hiring manager and the employee’s supervisor handle their parts; Simon Denby, Facilities and IT Coordinator, handles approved IT access.

## Before and at the start

1. The hiring manager gets headcount approval from Mara Bellamy, President and CEO. Bring Nia in on the approved hire and the employee’s role and supervisor. Don’t treat a request for headcount as an approval.

2. Nia gives the employee the required forms, the paper onboarding checklist and the handbook. The employee supplies identity and work-eligibility documents through the employer’s normal procedure, plus payroll and contact details. Nia follows up on missing items through that procedure. Do not write identity numbers into the sample data, and do not mark the paperwork complete just because forms were handed out. This procedure is not a guarantee of legal compliance; follow the applicable requirements for the particular hire.

3. Nia uses the payroll details for the separate payroll setup and keeps the paperwork securely. The office roster and payroll preparation use Excel; payroll is submitted through RUN Powered by ADP. Onboarding uses paper forms and a paper checklist. Keep the information available to the people who need it for their work, rather than putting sensitive documents in general sample files.

## Access and the first work

An approved access request is on paper. Send it to Simon for the access actually approved for the job. Simon provisions only approved IT access and maintains the Excel device and access list. The company uses Windows PCs and a shared office drive, but a hire does not automatically get access to every system or file. If a needed request is not approved yet, leave that access pending and get the approval rather than bypassing the request.

The supervisor introduces the employee to the site, conducts the safety training needed for the assigned tasks, and signs off. Nia records the completion on the onboarding checklist and retains the forms securely. If something is still outstanding, leave it open and follow up with the person responsible. Work requiring the task-specific training should wait until that training is done and signed off. Don’t substitute a handbook handout for the supervisor’s training and signoff.

For a shop-floor example of the reporting handoff, Fabrication Supervisor Theo Lowry supervises E021 Micah Orlin; Assembly Supervisor Avery Carver supervises the assembly and finish staff listed under Avery. Those are roster examples, not claims that either person is a new hire or that a particular onboarding was completed.

## Payroll handoff and follow-up

Make sure the employee knows which time record applies. At the snapshot Laurel Run has 50 employees: 36 hourly and 14 salaried. Hourly staff use paper cards for Sunday–Saturday workweeks; supervisors approve their direct reports’ cards and the relevant managers approve the other four hourly staff. Salaried staff submit paper exception notices, not hourly cards. Every 14 days Nia collects the cards, resolves omissions, enters approved weekly regular and overtime hours, reviews totals with Lydia Fenner, Controller, and submits payroll. There are 26 biweekly periods in a year.

The 2026-10-18 through 2026-10-31 hours sample illustrates the later payroll handoff, not a new-hire record: Theo’s approval of Micah’s card on 2026-11-02 and payday on 2026-11-05 were **planned**, not completed as of 2026-10-31.

If Nia is unavailable, the records do not name a standing onboarding backup; check with her manager Lydia about coverage and keep the forms secure. Simon also handles building issues. If access help is unavailable, keep the request pending rather than granting unapproved access. A supervisor reports an urgent hazard immediately to Owen Bellamy, Plant Manager, and takes unsafe equipment out of service; routine access or maintenance scheduling must not delay that response.
