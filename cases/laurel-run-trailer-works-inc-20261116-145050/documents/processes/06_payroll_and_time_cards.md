# Payroll and time cards

**Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc. — fictional procedure, as of 2026-10-31**

Nia Hadley (E005), Office and Payroll Administrator, collects the time information and prepares payroll. Lydia Fenner (E004), Controller, reviews the totals with her. We run a 14-day pay period: two separate Sunday–Saturday workweeks. There are 26 biweekly periods in a year. Keep the weeks separate when working out regular and overtime hours; the two-week total alone doesn't tell the whole story.

## Cards and signoff

The 36 hourly employees record their start and end times, breaks and weekly totals on paper cards. That includes 32 shop employees, E019–E050, counting both working supervisors, plus E006, E007, E014 and E015. The 14 salaried employees submit paper exception notices; they don't use the hourly cards for this routine. Pay basis by itself does not establish a person's legal overtime classification.

At the end of each period, get the cards to the right approver. Theo Lowry (E019), Fabrication Supervisor, signs for his direct reports, and Avery Carver (E020), Assembly Supervisor, signs for hers. Both also work with their crews and have their own hourly cards. Their manager, Plant Manager Owen Bellamy (E002), is the planned approver for those two cards in the sample period.

The other four hourly cards go to the relevant managers: Lydia for Accounting Clerk Elena Corwin (E006), Mara Bellamy (E001), President and CEO, for Reception and Admin Coordinator Holly Darnell (E007), and Materials and Purchasing Manager Devon Kersey (E012) for Receiving and Inventory Clerk Cora Whitlock (E014) and Shipping Coordinator Mateo Renshaw (E015). Then the approved cards go to Nia.

If a time, break, total or signature is missing or unclear, Nia checks with the employee and the relevant approver to resolve it. Send a corrected card back for the appropriate signoff. If it cannot be resolved, or Nia is away, bring it promptly to Lydia for direction; don't guess at the hours or treat a missing card as zero. The available records do not name a backup payroll submitter or an alternate approver.

## Prepare and review

Nia uses the office roster and Excel payroll preparation, collects the approved cards and paper exception notices, and enters the approved weekly regular and overtime amounts into **RUN Powered by ADP**. She checks the entries and totals against the cards, reviews payroll totals with Lydia, then submits payroll. Allow about two hours per period for Nia's hourly-card entry and verification; supervisor review and signoff are separate work.

Keep the approved paper cards and exception notices with the payroll preparation records for review, and handle them securely. The staff-hours file is an illustrative period summary, not the annual payroll ledger. Salaried hours in that sample are estimates, not hourly cards; blank regular or overtime fields do not settle legal classification.

## Example: period ending October 31

For the illustrative period **Sunday 2026-10-18 through Saturday 2026-10-31**, Fabricator/Welder Micah Orlin (E021) has 40 hours in the first week, October 18–24, and 42 in the second, October 25–31. The sample shows 40 regular and no overtime in week one, then 40 regular and two overtime in week two: 82 hours altogether.

At the sample $24 hourly rate, 80 regular hours × $24 plus two overtime hours × $36 gives **$1,992 gross, before withholding**. This is one fictional example, not a general legal payroll interpretation.
