# Marketing and social media — simulated interview

*Fictional academic case; this is a simulated text interview, not a record of an actual conversation. Snapshot: 2026-10-31. Intended duration: approximately 20 minutes, including natural pauses.*

**Interviewee:** Keira Morrow, Marketing and Dealer Support Coordinator  
**Topic:** Marketing materials, fictional company social channels, inquiries, dealer referrals and activity reporting

### [00:00]

**Interviewer:** Keira, could you start with your role and what marketing looks like at Laurel Run? I’m interested in the work as it actually comes across your desk, not just the channels.

**SME:** I’m the Marketing and Dealer Support Coordinator, and I report to Tessa Bellamy, our Dealer Sales Manager. My time is estimated at about 70 percent marketing, 15 percent customer service, 10 percent sales and 5 percent facilities and admin. Those are approximate shares, not four separate jobs. In marketing I work on product sheets and dealer handouts, the campaign calendar, social posts and the related records. I also communicate with dealers in Outlook.

**Interviewer:** Who is the audience? Is social mainly about selling a trailer directly?

**SME:** Laurel Run builds light open utility, enclosed cargo and equipment trailers, and sells through independent dealers rather than directly to retail customers. Someone may see company product information on the website, a printed handout, or a social post; dealers have their own customer relationships. On social, I can answer product or availability questions and help somebody find dealer contact information. The dealer sets the retail price and handles the retail purchase. I don’t quote a retail price.

**Interviewer:** How do the materials come together? Walk me through the sequence from an idea to something people see.

**SME:** I maintain an Excel campaign calendar and prepare product sheets and dealer handouts in Word. The social material generally consists of product photos, short product descriptions or dealer announcements, using approved product information and photos. Tessa approves marketing material, including posts, before publication. After her approval I publish the post and keep the approval with my working files. Printed handouts go to dealer visits, and I keep distribution and reply notes in a spreadsheet. Those are related activities, but a printed handout isn’t a social post.

**Interviewer:** If a draft hasn’t been approved, can you publish it? And who would approve it if Tessa were away?

**SME:** The stated sequence is Tessa’s approval before publication, then I publish. I wouldn’t treat a draft awaiting approval as published. As for a substitute approver or an exception procedure, I’m not sure; the records I have don’t establish one.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example of a product you might describe without suggesting a particular post contained it?

**SME:** Sure. The company website describes the Trailfield 6×12 as a baseline open utility trailer with a steel frame, open deck and rear loading gate. That’s a product-information example, not a claim that those words or that model appeared in a particular recorded post. I’d keep the description within the approved information; a dealer can discuss the configuration and retail price.

### [04:00]

**Interviewer:** What company social channels are we talking about, and what does the calendar actually tell us about their use?

**SME:** In this fictional case the company had Facebook and Instagram channels throughout the reporting year, November 1, 2025 through October 31, 2026. The values are invented, not results from real accounts. There was no company TikTok account during that year and no TikTok posts. The channel inventory doesn’t list a company LinkedIn or YouTube account; that isn’t evidence about an independent dealer’s channels. We aren’t given account handles or addresses.

**Interviewer:** How much material went out on the two channels?

**SME:** The year shows 18 Facebook posts in ten months and seven Instagram posts in six months. Each of those seven Instagram posts used a topic and photo that also appeared on Facebook in the same month. If one appears on both channels, it is one post in each channel count. It isn’t seven additional topics on top of everything on Facebook. On the other hand, those counts alone don’t tell us exactly what every post said.

**Interviewer:** Can we look at a month to make that distinction concrete?

**SME:** October 2026 shows two Facebook posts and one Instagram post, with one shared topic-and-photo pair. So the one Instagram post is included in the Instagram total and its paired Facebook post is included in the Facebook total. We don’t have the individual post texts or photos in the monthly file. I can tell you the relationship between the counts, not reconstruct the content.

**Interviewer:** And the gaps? Was the plan to post more often, or did something interrupt the schedule?

**SME:** I’m not sure why the gaps occurred… or what posting frequency was targeted. The record establishes that Facebook had no posts in January and July 2026. Instagram had no posts in December 2025 and in January, March, May, July and September 2026. Those are recorded post counts, not reasons. The accounts were still present, and a month without a post isn’t necessarily a month without an inquiry.

**Interviewer:** Were those channels reaching more people over the year?

**SME:** At the October snapshot the fictional Facebook page had 248 followers and Instagram had 73. Those are separate account snapshots, not a count of distinct people across both channels. The monthly sheet doesn’t systematically retain organic reach, impressions, engagement, video views, website-traffic source or link clicks. So I can report the follower figures and posts, but I can’t tell you from this sheet how many people saw a post or followed a link. Organic reach and a later conversion are different questions, too.

### [08:00]

**Interviewer:** Let’s follow an inquiry. What triggers your work, and where does it start?

**SME:** I check the company’s two social accounts and answer product or availability questions there. If someone asks where to buy, I give current dealer contact information from the company dealer list. With the person’s permission, I can also forward their contact and inquiry to a dealer through Outlook. Those are two different actions: giving the person a dealer contact and forwarding the person’s information to the dealer.

**Interviewer:** Suppose someone asks for product information but doesn’t ask where to buy. What happens to that count?

**SME:** As an example, a qualifying product-information message thread belongs in the inquiry count, even if no dealer contact is given. For the reporting year there were two threads that asked only for product information, and those weren’t given dealer contact information. The file doesn’t provide the message contents or customer identities, so I can’t quote either conversation.

**Interviewer:** If a person writes again in the same conversation, do you count another inquiry? What about a comment under a post?

**SME:** The unit is a separate new inbound product- or availability-related message thread. Later messages in an existing thread aren’t new inquiry threads. Comments, spam and dealer operational correspondence aren’t part of that count either. And the thread count isn’t necessarily a count of distinct people. The monthly ledger is complete for the specified channels and recorded fields, not for every interaction or every marketing outcome.

**Interviewer:** How quickly did people hear back?

**SME:** The fictional records show 13 qualifying Facebook threads and four Instagram threads in the year. All 17 received an initial reply within two business days, and by the snapshot. A business day here means Monday through Friday, excluding company holidays. That’s a count of timely first replies to those threads; it doesn’t say every subsequent question was resolved in that period. I’m not sure whether a separate response-time target existed; the recorded measure doesn’t establish one.

**Interviewer:** And after that first reply, how many were sent onward?

**SME:** Dealer contact information was given in 15 of the 17 threads: 11 on Facebook and four on Instagram. Six of those 15 were also forwarded to a dealer with the person’s permission: five from Facebook and one from Instagram. Please don’t add the six forwards to the 15; they are a subset. We don’t have a documented dealer follow-through or retail outcome in the recap. Forwarding is a handoff, not proof that a visit or purchase happened.

### [12:00]

**Interviewer:** I’d like to see the handoff in one month. What can the ledger support without filling in the missing story?

**SME:** In October 2026 there were two Facebook inquiry threads and one Instagram thread. All three had an initial reply within two business days, and all three were given dealer contact information. One of the Facebook threads was forwarded with permission; none of the October Instagram threads was recorded as forwarded. The counted replies, contact information and forward occurred in the same month as those inquiries. We can’t identify the people or tell from that row which dealer was involved, what was said, or whether a sale followed.

**Interviewer:** If someone asks you for a price in a message, is the dealer contact just an extra step after you quote it?

**SME:** No. I don’t quote a retail price. Independent dealers set their own retail prices and handle retail purchases. The website also directs people to a dealer for configurations, options and current availability. I can provide approved product information, but I wouldn’t turn the company’s dealer-side figures into a promised retail quote. The monthly data don’t say which specific questions were asked in October.

**Interviewer:** What if a person wants a dealer’s details but hasn’t given permission for their own contact to be forwarded?

**SME:** The distinction matters. The process says I give current dealer contact information when someone asks where to buy, and forward their contact and inquiry with their permission. Giving them a dealer’s information isn’t the same as sending their information to that dealer. The annual counts show that not every thread given dealer information was forwarded. I’m not sure of the reason for any individual non-forward; the aggregate doesn’t say.

**Interviewer:** July is interesting because there were no posts. Was there anything to handle?

**SME:** Yes. In July 2026 there were no posts on either channel, but the sheet records one Facebook product or availability inquiry. It received a timely initial reply and dealer contact information; it wasn’t recorded as forwarded. There was also a monthly recap, recorded at 25 minutes. I can’t tell from the row what caused the inquiry or whether it related to an earlier post. Having an active account and having a post that month aren’t the same thing.

**Interviewer:** Are dealer emails and these messages managed in one complete customer-history system?

**SME:** I work with Outlook, the dealer list, social accounts and my Excel records. The monthly social recap brings together counts from the platforms and related correspondence, but it isn’t a complete customer history or a record of later dealer outcomes. I’m not sure how any particular dealer manages its own follow-up, and I wouldn’t infer it from our counts.

### [16:00]

**Interviewer:** Take me through the month-end recap itself. What do you look at, and where do the numbers go?

**SME:** I review the two channels and related saved correspondence, then copy and reconcile the counts in Excel and file a monthly recap for Tessa. The sheet covers posts, month-end follower snapshots, qualifying incoming threads, timely initial replies, dealer contact information given and consented forwards. The campaign calendar and the distribution and reply notes support other parts of the work; the recap is the monthly social activity record. Tessa is my manager and approves marketing material. The file doesn’t give me a detailed procedure for her review of each recap.

**Interviewer:** What sort of checking is involved when there are several actions on one inquiry?

**SME:** The important thing is to keep the categories straight while reconciling the channels and correspondence. An initial reply is part of an inquiry’s history; a dealer contact can be given for that same thread; and a consented forward can be a further action on a subset of those contacts. They don’t become three new leads. Likewise, if a topic and photo were posted on both channels, each channel has its own post count, while the shared pair tells us how those posts relate. The sheet’s counts don’t establish a person-level journey.

**Interviewer:** How much time does that reporting take, and does the number include creating or answering everything?

**SME:** The 12 recorded monthly recaps total 540 minutes. The entries are for reviewing the accounts and correspondence, copying and reconciling counts, and filing the recap. They exclude creating posts, obtaining approval, answering and forwarding inquiries, and preparing handouts. There’s an entry even in a month without posts: January 2026, for example, records no posts on either channel and a 15-minute recap. October’s recap is 60 minutes, and the October sheet is current through the snapshot. Those numbers are not the total time spent on marketing.

**Interviewer:** What can Tessa reasonably conclude from the recap, and where does your confidence stop?

**SME:** She can see the recorded monthly activity and the defined response and referral counts. She can’t use that sheet to establish organic reach, link clicks, later dealer follow-through, retail conversions, or manufacturer orders or revenue attributed to social. Missing outcomes don’t mean zero outcomes. I find the point after a referral frustrating because I can describe what I sent, but… not tell you from the recap what happened next. I’m not sure how many people who received a dealer contact bought a trailer.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you want to leave us with about interpreting the numbers?

**SME:** Just keep the boundaries visible. A referral is not a purchase, and a dealer’s retail sale is not Laurel Run’s sale to the dealer. The follower counts aren’t unique combined people, and a quiet posting month can still contain a question and a recap. We have useful activity counts, but not every measure someone might want to ask for.

**Interviewer:** That’s clear. Thank you, Keira. We’ll close there, at approximately 20 minutes.
