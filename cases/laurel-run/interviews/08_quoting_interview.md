# Laurel Run Trailer Works — Quoting Interview

*Fictional, simulated interview; not a recording or account of an actual conversation.*  
**Interviewee:** Tessa Bellamy, Dealer Sales Manager  
**Case snapshot:** 2026-10-31  
**Topic:** Dealer quoting and the handoff to an accepted order  
**Intended duration:** Approximately 20 minutes, including natural pauses

[00:00]

**Interviewer:** To begin, could you tell me what quoting covers for you and where you fit in the work?

**SME:** We quote independent dealers, not the person who eventually buys the trailer at retail. I manage dealer sales and report to Mara Bellamy. Priya Ellwood is our Dealer Account Coordinator and reports to me. She handles the incoming requests and can prepare a routine quote in the Excel template. I maintain the pricing and formula workbook, answer the unusual pricing or formula questions, and approve the quotes. After my approval, Priya sends the dated quote to the dealer.

**Interviewer:** What starts a request, and what does Priya need before there is a usable quote?

**SME:** It can come through Outlook or a call. Holly Darnell routes calls and takes handwritten messages when needed. Priya checks the dealer ID against the Excel dealer list and captures the model, dimensions, requested options and quantity in the quote template. Those details matter because the dealer needs to see what the price is actually for. A model name on its own doesn't tell you the options or how many trailers are requested.

**Interviewer:** What would you do if the request isn't clear about one of those details?

**SME:** We need the detail rather than a guess. Take an option: a spare-tire mount is not a spare tire. If the request doesn't establish what the dealer wants, it needs clarification before we can treat a particular configuration as the quote. I'm not sure what the average time is to get an answer, or whether anyone tracks that separately. I wouldn't put a number on it.

**Interviewer:** Is the dealer list the whole contact history?

**SME:** No. The dealer list and Outlook are the working contact records. The list is a dealer account list, not a complete contact file or a list of retail customers. There are 80 active independent dealer accounts in the snapshot. We have Zoho CRM licensed for three users, but Priya has entered only a small pilot set of contacts. You can't assume the CRM contains every dealer contact just because the licenses exist.

**Interviewer:** So if the request begins with a call rather than a message, is the paper note itself the approval?

**SME:** No. A handwritten message can help route the request. It isn't my quote approval or the dealer's written acceptance. Those are separate points. The dealer's written approval is retained with the Outlook email; a call note doesn't establish acceptance.

[04:00]

**Interviewer:** Take me through the pricing work. What can Priya do herself, and what comes to you?

**SME:** Priya can fill in the routine template using the requested configuration. I own the workbook and understand its formula and rate logic; using the template doesn't make Priya the formula owner. She sends exceptional pricing or formula questions to me. I handle approximately 100 of those questions a year. That's a count of questions, not a count of all quotes, dealers or trailers.

**Interviewer:** How do you decide what is included in a routine price?

**SME:** Start with the relevant dealer base and add only the options the dealer requested. The standard fictional dealer base examples are $4,800 for the 6 by 12 Trailfield, $9,600 for the 7 by 14 Enclosure, and $12,000 for the 82-inch by 18-foot Workspan, before options. A spare-tire mount only is $120, an upgraded jack is $250, and an interior tie-down package is $180. Those are examples, not the entire option catalog. And again, the mount doesn't include the tire.

**Interviewer:** If Priya can't resolve a formula question and you're unavailable, can another person approve the price to keep things moving?

**SME:** The quote stays pending for me if the answer or my approval isn't available. We don't invent a price, and Priya filling the template isn't a substitute for my approval. [Pause.] I can't tell you from these records how long an individual pending question waits or give you a service target for resolving it. I'm not sure.

**Interviewer:** What does your approval establish, and how is it different from the dealer's acceptance?

**SME:** My approval allows Priya to send the dated quote to the dealer. The quote receives an identifier from the `QTE-YYYY-NNNN` sequence. At that point it is an offer; it isn't an order just because I've approved it or because it has been sent. The dealer must accept in writing. Their written approval stays with the Outlook email. I'm not sure the records specify an exact method for recording my internal approval, so I wouldn't describe a particular button, signature or form.

**Interviewer:** If the dealer tells a retail customer a different price, is that a change to your quote?

**SME:** Not necessarily. Our quote is a dealer price. Independent dealers set their own retail prices. We don't have their subsequent retail prices in these records, and I can't derive one by adding a presumed markup. Nor would an offer we made be shipment revenue simply because it has a dollar value on it.

**Interviewer:** Do you have a standard expiration period or an automatic discount rule you can point to?

**SME:** I'm not sure. The supplied record tells us the quote is dated and describes exceptional questions coming to me. It doesn't establish a general expiration period or a discount threshold. I wouldn't fill either in from habit or assume Priya can approve an exception.

[08:00]

**Interviewer:** Could we walk through a real example from the fictional case, from the dealer request onward?

**SME:** Yes. Cedar Bend Trailer Sales, dealer `DLR-0001`, requested quote `QTE-2026-1180` on 2026-10-14. It was for one 6 by 12 Trailfield with the spare-tire mount only. Priya prepared it and I approved the price: $4,800 for the base and $120 for the mount, or $4,920. That's the dealer total, not Cedar Bend's retail price.

**Interviewer:** What do we know about the dealer's response and the next record?

**SME:** Cedar Bend accepted in writing on 2026-10-15. That led to `ORD-2026-1120`, a separate order record, and `WO-2026-1240`, the separate work order for that one trailer. Both are dated 2026-10-15 in the example. Jonah Sutter was the scheduler. The letters matter: quote, order and work order are different sequences. You shouldn't infer that matching or similar suffixes join them, or infer a VIN from a work-order number.

**Interviewer:** Why does Priya need to revisit the approved quote at the order stage?

**SME:** She checks the dealer ID and the approved quote, then starts the order in the Excel order register. She re-keys the approved model and options into the register and the paper work-order build packet. So the approved detail has to remain visible through that handoff. Jonah assigns a separate work-order identifier for each trailer and a target date, then schedules the work. The order record and the paper packet are separate from the original quote and email.

**Interviewer:** Does that target date mean you can promise the dealer delivery on that date?

**SME:** No. A scheduling target is an estimate, not a guarantee. That's sometimes a difficult distinction to explain when a dealer wants certainty. If the dealer asks about current status, Priya checks with Jonah or Mateo Renshaw. I wouldn't use the existence of a scheduled work order to promise that the unit has shipped.

**Interviewer:** What happened later in the Cedar Bend example, by the snapshot?

**SME:** Finish inspection was 2026-10-26. The trailer shipped and was invoiced on 2026-10-27, as `INV-2026-1161`, for $4,920. Cedar Bend is recorded as approved for credit, so the terms were net 30 from the shipment invoice. The stated amount excludes sales tax and has no separate freight charge. Those shipment and invoice events had occurred by 2026-10-31; the earlier quote or acceptance shouldn't be presented as if either one was the shipment.

**Interviewer:** Do we know what Cedar Bend charged its own buyer, or whether it had sold the trailer by the snapshot?

**SME:** I'm not sure. The example gives the manufacturer-to-dealer sequence, not the dealer's retail price or a subsequent retail sale. We shouldn't attach an outcome that isn't in the record.

[12:00]

**Interviewer:** Let's go back to that handoff. What should someone compare if they are trying to understand whether the build reflects what was accepted?

**SME:** Follow the dealer, model and options from the approved quote and written acceptance into the order register and paper build packet. In the example, that's one Trailfield with the mount only, not a tire or another option. The records show the chain, not a detailed log of every check performed.

**Interviewer:** Suppose the specification needs to change after the dealer has accepted. Who has to be involved?

**SME:** I obtain the dealer's approval for the change. Jonah issues a dated replacement packet page, and Theo Lowry and Avery Carver return the old page. Iris Calder checks the current revision. The important distinction is that a proposed change isn't the same as an approved specification. Don't make an unapproved substitution.

**Interviewer:** Is sales approval enough if the proposed change is a component substitution?

**SME:** No. Felix Harrow, our Manufacturing Engineer, and Iris, our Quality and Warranty Lead, must confirm fit and required specifications. Brakes, tires, couplers and lighting also need fit and applicable compliance review. That is not an invitation for me to promise a legal conclusion on a call. And a component or axle rating by itself doesn't establish the finished trailer's payload; the completed trailer's certification and load label gives its GVWR and payload.

**Interviewer:** Are you describing a change that occurred on Cedar Bend's trailer?

**SME:** No. I'm describing the process for a proposed change. The example doesn't establish that there was a substitution or a specification change on that trailer. We should keep the procedure separate from the completed example.

**Interviewer:** What about credit? If the account exists on the dealer list, can the standard terms go on any quote?

**SME:** The standard terms are net 30 days from the shipment invoice for dealers approved for credit. An active account and approved credit aren't the same fact. Cedar Bend is listed as approved; the dealer list has other entries where credit is simply not specified. That doesn't mean those dealers have been declined, and it doesn't mean they've been approved either. I'm not sure of an unlisted account's credit status from a blank or unspecified field.

**Interviewer:** Who actually approves credit, or what should happen if that status needs to be established?

**SME:** I'm not sure from the quoting record. I can describe the condition for the stated terms, but I can't assign someone an approval authority or claim a credit decision took place without a record of it. We'd need the applicable information before stating that the approved-credit terms apply.

[16:00]

**Interviewer:** Where do you see the practical effort in managing a request from the dealer's first contact through the order?

**SME:** Details are in different places. Priya may start with Outlook or a routed call and the dealer list, then use the quote template. I have the pricing and formula workbook. After written acceptance, she re-enters the approved details in the order register and the paper packet, while Jonah works with a separate schedule. To answer a question about the request, the acceptance or the build, the particular record matters. A quote number is useful, but it isn't the order number or the work-order number.

**Interviewer:** What happens to the written acceptance when you move on to the order?

**SME:** It's retained with the Outlook email. The order register is separate; entering an order isn't the dealer's written acceptance. The paper packet represents the build handoff, not a substitute for my approval. I'm not sure where every attachment is filed; the record establishes retention with the email.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned roughly 100 exceptional pricing or formula questions a year. What else can you tell me about volume and timing?

**SME:** The reporting year has 1,500 completed and shipped trailers, and each trailer has its own work order. But those units aren't a count of quotes, and the 100 questions aren't a count of quotes either. I'm not sure how many quotes we issued, how many dealers declined, how many went unanswered, or how long quoting took on average. One completed example cannot answer those questions for the year.

**Interviewer:** Can we calculate a quote-to-order conversion rate from the Cedar Bend record and the shipment total?

**SME:** No. Cedar Bend is one illustrative linked example, and the shipment total is a unit count. We don't have a complete quote population and its outcomes in these materials. Missing outcomes aren't zero. We also don't have the number of trailers represented by every quote, so the two figures aren't interchangeable.

**Interviewer:** If you wanted to explain a delay to a dealer, how much could you say from the records we have here?

**SME:** I could say a quote is pending if my answer or approval isn't available. For an order's current status, Priya checks with Jonah or Mateo; a target isn't a delivery guarantee. I couldn't give a measured delay, identify a particular missed date or say how often that happens from the information we have. [Pause.] There's a difference between explaining the handoff and claiming a history we haven't been shown.

**Interviewer:** And the clearest boundary at the end of quoting?

**SME:** My approval makes the dated quote ready to send; the dealer's written acceptance is what allows Priya to start the separate order record. Then the approved configuration has to carry forward into the build packet. If a later change is proposed, get the required dealer and technical approvals rather than treating the proposal as already accepted. What happens at retail is the dealer's business, and we don't have that outcome here.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Tessa. That brings us to about twenty minutes.

**SME:** You're welcome.
