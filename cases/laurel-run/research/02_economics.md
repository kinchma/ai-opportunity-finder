# Public economics benchmarks for an invented U.S. utility/cargo trailer maker

**Bottom line:** In 2022, the closest size-specific federal proxy implies approximately **$272,000 of annual receipts per paid employee** for enterprises with fewer than 20 employees, and **$255,000** for those with 20–49 employees. These are aggregate ratios for an industry that also includes campers, not actual results for a fictional company. [\[1\]](https://data.census.gov/profile/336214_-_Travel_Trailer_and_Camper_Manufacturing?g=010XX00US&codeset=naics~336214) [\[2\]](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_naics_detailedsizes_2022.txt) [\[3\]](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/about/glossary.html)

## Classification and revenue per employee

A light utility or automobile trailer establishment is generally included in **NAICS 336214, Travel Trailer and Camper Manufacturing**, which also contains travel trailers, campers and pickup caps. **NAICS 336212, Truck Trailer Manufacturing**, instead covers truck trailers, detachable bodies and chassis; its examples include commercial flatbeds, dump trailers and semitrailers. The proper classification depends on the establishment’s principal activity and product, so a generic “cargo trailer” label alone is insufficient. Neither category isolates small non-RV utility/cargo manufacturers. [\[1\]](https://data.census.gov/profile/336214_-_Travel_Trailer_and_Camper_Manufacturing?g=010XX00US&codeset=naics~336214) [\[4\]](https://www.census.gov/naics/?input=336212&year=2022&details=336212)

The Census Bureau’s 2022 Statistics of U.S. Businesses, or SUSB, data use 2017 NAICS codes and classify observations by **enterprise-wide employment size**, while reporting establishments in each industry. Receipts are in thousands of dollars and are annual; employment is the count of full- and part-time paid employees in the pay period including March 12, **not annual-average headcount or full-time equivalents**. The ratios below are total receipts divided by total employees, not median company revenue per employee: [\[2\]](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_naics_detailedsizes_2022.txt) [\[3\]](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/about/glossary.html)

- **336214, fewer than 20 employees:** 360 firms; $636.684 million receipts and 2,343 paid employees. Calculation: $636,684,000 / 2,343 = **$271,739 per employee**.
- **336214, 20–49 employees:** summing the published 20–24, 25–29, 30–34, 35–39 and 40–49 enterprise-size rows yields $686.785 million receipts and 2,691 employees. Calculation: $686,785,000 / 2,691 = **$255,216 per employee**.
- For scale, **all 336214 sizes** yielded $30.741107 billion / 66,206 = **$464,325 per employee**. The large gap shows why a small-firm bracket matters; it is not an isolated utility-trailer benchmark.
- A more distant **336212, fewer-than-20-employee** comparison was $535.082 million / 1,376 = **$388,868 per employee**; the products and customer base are different. [\[2\]](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_naics_detailedsizes_2022.txt)

SUSB excludes nonemployer businesses and the denominator excludes proprietors and partners of unincorporated businesses. The figures are nominal 2022 dollars, and summed establishment receipts can duplicate some transactions within larger multi-establishment businesses. Census applied disclosure-avoidance noise: the under-20 336214 receipt and employment cells are flagged **G** for less than 2% noise; the 20–49 calculation includes employment cells flagged **H**, or 2% to less than 5% noise. Do not treat the last digits, or the range between these two averages, as precision or as a statistical distribution of small utility-only makers. [\[2\]](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_naics_detailedsizes_2022.txt) [\[3\]](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/about/glossary.html)

## Gross and operating margins

There is no verified, publicly disclosed small-utility-trailer-only margin average in these sources. Two **actual but imperfect comparators**, both much larger and with different product mixes, illustrate magnitude and cyclicality:

- **Winnebago Industries, fiscal year ended August 29, 2026:** consolidated net revenue $2,703.9 million, cost of goods sold $2,353.7 million, gross profit $350.2 million and operating income $66.2 million. Gross margin = $350.2m / $2,703.9m = **13.0%**; operating margin = $66.2m / $2,703.9m = **2.4%** as reported. This is a diversified RV and marine company, not a cargo/utility-only manufacturer. Its towable RV segment separately reported 4.5% operating margin, but a segment result does not equal whole-company margin and may not include all corporate costs. [\[5\]](https://winnebago.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/winnebago-industries-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2)
- **Wabash National, calendar 2025:** $1,542.754 million net sales and $69.911 million gross profit imply **4.5% gross margin**. Reported GAAP operating income of $307.505 million implies **19.9%**; the company’s reconciled, non-GAAP adjusted operating loss of $94.943 million implies **−6.2%**. A $418.594 million adjustment for a Missouri legal matter and a $16.146 million impairment adjustment make the reported and adjusted figures materially different. In 2024, gross margin was $265.072m / $1,946.740m = **13.6%**, GAAP operating margin **−18.3%**, and adjusted operating margin **4.8%**; the latter removes a $450 million legal charge. In 2023, gross margin was **19.6%** and GAAP operating margin was **12.3%**. These are company-specific observations, not an industry range, and Wabash primarily makes large commercial trailers, truck bodies and related parts/services. [\[6\]](https://ir.onewabash.com/news/news-details/2026/Wabash-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx) [\[7\]](https://ir.onewabash.com/news/news-details/2025/Wabash-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024-Results/default.aspx) [\[8\]](https://s25.q4cdn.com/609616967/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Wabash-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)

Gross margin is gross profit divided by sales, after cost of sales; operating margin is operating income divided by sales, after operating expenses and before interest and income taxes. Both vary with the company’s cost classifications. Adjusted operating margin is non-GAAP and should not be conflated with reported GAAP profit. None of these results is evidence of the invented manufacturer’s revenue or profit. [\[5\]](https://winnebago.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/winnebago-industries-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2) [\[6\]](https://ir.onewabash.com/news/news-details/2026/Wabash-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx) [\[7\]](https://ir.onewabash.com/news/news-details/2025/Wabash-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024-Results/default.aspx)

## Purchasing and sales patterns

**Inputs:** In its 2025 annual report, Wabash said it purchased specialty steel coil, stainless steel, plastic, aluminum, lumber, tires, landing gear, axles and suspensions from a limited number of suppliers. It described fixed-price supplier contracts and commodity hedges, and said cost increases are not always passed through immediately. That is evidence for a large commercial-trailer producer, not proof that a small shop has the same contracts, hedges, supplier count or cost mix. [\[8\]](https://s25.q4cdn.com/609616967/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Wabash-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)

For the actual light-trailer market, Lippert publicly offers utility/cargo-trailer axles and towing components including jacks, couplers and safety chains, as well as cargo doors. These are offered products—not supplier quotations, purchase orders or evidence that any invented business buys from Lippert. [\[9\]](https://corporate.lippert.com/products/utility-trailer)

**Customers and channels:** Cynergy Cargo says its Georgia enclosed-cargo operation sells only to approved dealers, which can order basic units or customized trailers. Diamond C says it distributes open steel trailers through more than 100 dealer partners in North America. These are examples of observed manufacturer-to-dealer channels, not evidence of typical order frequency, margins, payment terms, or the invented company’s customers. [\[10\]](https://cynergycargo.com/) [\[11\]](https://www.diamondc.com/)

**Illustrative assumptions only:** For scenario construction, one could use **$250,000–$275,000 annual receipts per paid employee in 2022 dollars**, a **15% gross margin** and a **3% operating margin**; also test a negative operating-margin case. These are selected modeling inputs inspired by imperfect proxies, not measured utility-only averages, forecasts, price quotes or a statement about a real or invented firm. A workable fictional purchasing pattern could be recurring orders of steel and standardized axle/towing components, plus order-specific enclosed-body items; frequency, lot sizes, lead times, discounts and payment terms remain unsupported assumptions rather than documented facts.
