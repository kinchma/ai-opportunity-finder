# Marketing and social media: research notes

**Bottom line:** A light social presence is plausible for a small dealer-channel manufacturer, but the available evidence does not establish that weak social media is typical of 50-person Pennsylvania trailer makers or that it causes a particular level of lost sales. Manufacturers and dealers can use websites, social posts, video and in-person relationships for different stages of the buying process. [\[1\]](https://www.straightnorth.com/blog/manufacturer-marketing-survey-2025/) [\[2\]](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/research/manufacturing-content-marketing-challenges-research) [\[3\]](https://belmonttrailers.com/)

## What the evidence supports

**Small manufacturers use a range of approaches.** In a digital agency’s 2025 report on 114 U.S. manufacturing respondents, 70% represented companies with fewer than 100 employees. Ten percent of respondents reported no investment in digital marketing; others reported investing in email, search optimization and other channels. This is an agency-produced survey of respondents, with recruitment and representativeness not established in its published write-up. No digital investment is not the same thing as having no website or social accounts. [\[1\]](https://www.straightnorth.com/blog/manufacturer-marketing-survey-2025/)

A separate 2026 online panel survey of 517 small-business owners with 2–99 employees reported that 64% used social-media marketing and 58% used word-of-mouth referrals. It included many kinds of businesses, not just manufacturers; 62% had 20 or fewer employees. Panel participation can select for digitally reachable and willing respondents. These figures show a combination of approaches in that sample, not the prevalence for trailer manufacturers. [\[4\]](https://sbecouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/SBE-Technology-Use-Survey-March-2026-Final-2.pdf)

**Content and platforms.** Content Marketing Institute and MarketingProfs surveyed 104 manufacturing marketers worldwide in June–August 2024. In that content-marketing-oriented respondent group, 95% reported distributing content through organic social media and 85% reported using video in the prior 12 months. Respondents also cited LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok as providing the best value to their organizations, at 85%, 40%, 31%, 18% and 5%, respectively. These are perceived-value responses, not usage rates, audited sales or a channel recipe. Only 19% of respondents worked at firms with 1–99 employees, while 50% worked at firms with 1,000 or more; the sample is not representative of small U.S. trailer makers, and self-selection toward active marketers is a material concern. [\[2\]](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/research/manufacturing-content-marketing-challenges-research)

**Weak or absent presence can occur, but its frequency is unknown here.** A 2013 mail survey of U.S. forest-products businesses found 58% of its 166 responding firms used some form of social media for marketing; Facebook was the most common platform. Its reported response rate was 16.53%, and nonresponse may affect the result despite the authors’ checks. The sector is different and the data are old, so this is historical evidence of variation, not a present-day trailer-industry benchmark. [\[5\]](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/53739.pdf) The surveys above do not justify claims that family ownership, an owner’s age, or rural location explains any particular business’s digital activity.

## How a dealer channel can work

Belmont Trailers, a family-owned Pennsylvania manufacturer, presents its trailer models and a dealer locator on its website. Its media page offers product brochures and walk-around videos. A Pennsylvania dealer listed by Belmont, T.P. Trailers, separately promotes Belmont’s FlatTrak product and links its own Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube accounts. These are observed examples of separate manufacturer and dealer marketing, not evidence of average company size, posting frequency or effectiveness. The real websites were checked November 9, 2026, after the fictional company’s October snapshot, and can change. [\[3\]](https://belmonttrailers.com/) [\[6\]](https://belmonttrailers.com/media/) [\[7\]](https://www.tptrailersinc.com/flattrak-by-belmont-for-sale-limerick-pa) [\[11\]](https://belmonttrailers.com/dealer-locator/)

Diamond C, a different family-owned trailer manufacturer, links Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube from its site. Its dealer page directs buyers to local dealers and notes that not all dealers participate in its linked inventory program. A website link to a platform establishes an account link, not posting activity or performance. The example supports the possibility of multi-platform promotion alongside dealer sales, not a claim that this mix is typical. [\[8\]](https://www.diamondc.com/find-a-dealer/)

For a dealer-only manufacturer, a product post or video can build awareness and prompt someone to consult a dealer. A dealer referral or dealer-locator click is not a confirmed retail sale. A dealer’s eventual retail sale is also distinct from the manufacturer’s wholesale order and shipment to the dealer. NIST’s Manufacturing Extension Partnership discusses relationship-building and in-person networking alongside digital outreach; this is practitioner guidance, not a quantified attribution study. [\[9\]](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/manufacturing-innovation-blog/marketing-strategies-manufacturers-how-building-community-helps)

## Final fictional company scenario

**All Laurel Run details and company-specific results are synthetic, not sourced.** Laurel Run Trailer Works is an invented 50-employee family-owned Pennsylvania manufacturer selling through approximately 80 independent dealers. Its fictional website and dealer handouts sit alongside Facebook and Instagram accounts. During the reporting year the Facebook page had 18 posts in 10 months and Instagram had seven posts in six months; no TikTok account existed. Keira Morrow prepares and publishes approved content. The company channel inventory does not list a LinkedIn or YouTube account. These are case choices, not evidence-based norms.

The fictional activity record shows 17 product or availability inquiry threads, 15 supplied with dealer contact details and six of those forwarded with permission. The company does not systematically record reach, clicks, later dealer follow-through or social-attributed sales; a missing outcome is unknown, not zero. Dealers set retail prices and handle retail purchases, while Laurel Run records its own wholesale dealer shipments. For the complete definitions and the finalized fictional evidence, see [the company marketing overview](../company/04_marketing_social_media.md) and [the monthly activity record](../data/marketing_social_activity.csv). The researched examples above concern other real companies and do not establish Laurel Run's results.
