# Inventory counting

**Laurel Run Trailer Works, Inc. — fictional procedure, as of 2026-10-31**

Cora Whitlock, Receiving and Inventory Clerk, does the weekly count. Devon Kersey, Materials and Purchasing Manager, owns the inventory and purchasing records and reviews what we have and what is coming. The working stock list is in Excel on the shared office drive. QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise is used for supplier bills, not as the working shop-floor count.

## Each week

1. Cora hand-counts six selected, frequently used SKU categories and writes the counts on paper first. The six lines are selected stock, not the whole inventory. The routine runs over roughly 50 working weeks in the year. A workload estimate for counting and interpreting the selected lines is about 1½ hours in a working week.
2. Compare each physical quantity to the Excel stock-sheet quantity before the count. Use the right unit: axles are each, steel is counted in lengths, mounted wheels in assemblies and floor screws in boxes. The variance is the physical count less the pre-count sheet quantity.
3. Cora updates the Excel inventory sheet with the physical count. Keep the paper count with the inventory records and show the date, item, unit, before count, physical count, variance and updated balance in the count record. Devon reviews the count, including any difference. Don't treat an order that hasn't arrived as stock already on hand.
4. Devon compares the updated on-hand quantities with open purchase orders and likely near-term requirements. He uses that information and his experience to decide order quantities. Jonah Sutter, Production Scheduler, uses stock notes and supervisor input when sequencing work; Theo Lowry and Avery Carver report material exceptions from fabrication and assembly.

For a purchase, Devon approves Jalen Sorrell's PO and quantity; Jalen, the Buyer, assigns the PO number and emails it to the supplier. At receiving, Cora counts goods against the PO and signs the packing slip. She sends damage or a short receipt to Jalen before updating the stock sheet. Elena Corwin matches the bill, signed slip and PO; Devon approves exceptions.

For example, PO-2026-0237 was ordered on 2026-10-08 and all 50 SKU-0001 axles were received from Ridge Hollow Components LLC on 2026-10-15. The price was $276 each, delivered excluding sales tax. This is one monthly release, not a full purchasing ledger.

## When something is short

Tell Devon about the material exception and make sure Jonah gets the floor report for the schedule. If the needed material is not there, hold that work and use other work when available. Four separate shortages were recorded in the reporting year: SKU-0004 on 2025-12-03, SKU-0005 on 2026-03-18, SKU-0007 on 2026-06-17 and SKU-0012 on 2026-09-16. In each, three workers were held for three hours before reassignment to other work. These were not records of a whole-plant stop; no substitution or safety compromise occurred.

Do not substitute material without Felix Harrow, Manufacturing Engineer, and Iris Calder, Quality and Warranty Lead, confirming fit and required specifications. Dimensions, capacity, electrical details and bolt pattern matter as applicable. An axle rating alone does not establish the trailer's payload; check the completed trailer's certification and load labels. No assumed substitute or unreceived delivery is a reason to bypass the check.
