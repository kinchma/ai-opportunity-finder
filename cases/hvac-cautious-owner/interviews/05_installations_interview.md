# Installations - Interview with Ben Okonkwo

Interviewee: Ben Okonkwo, Install Manager (since 2014)
Date: 2026-10-29
Fictional transcript. About 19 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] Role

**Interviewer:** Ben, tell me about your role.

**SME:** I run installations. Two crews, eight installers. Ray Mendez leads crew 1 and Owen Fisk leads crew 2. I schedule the installs, get the equipment ordered through Lisa, pull the permits, do walk-throughs and final checks, and register the equipment for warranty. I report to Kyle. I've been here since 2014, I was an installer before that.

**Interviewer:** What were the numbers this year?

**SME:** 310 residential installs and 40 commercial rooftop replacements. Residential is usually one install per crew per day. The RTUs need a crane, so those I schedule with Wes and the crane company, and it's usually crew 2 with Sam or Hector helping on the controls.

**Interviewer:** How does a job reach you?

**SME:** Nadia sells it. When she gets the signed proposal and the deposit, it goes in a paper install folder, a manila folder, and comes to me. I log it in the install log spreadsheet, Install Log 2026, with the JOB number, customer, equipment and the install date.

## [02:30] Equipment and the morning pickup

**Interviewer:** How does the equipment get to the job?

**SME:** I give Lisa the equipment list for each job and she orders from Northgate. Then Ray picks it up at the Northgate counter the morning of the install. He's there when they open, loads the truck, and heads to the job. It's about 40 minutes each way from our shop, plus loading, so figure an hour and a half most mornings before crew 1 is on site.

**Interviewer:** Is that every install day?

**SME:** Pretty much, for crew 1. Crew 2 sometimes gets their equipment from our warehouse if Lisa has it in stock. Ray picks up for both crews a lot of the time, he just loads more.

**Interviewer:** Does Northgate deliver?

**SME:** They do. Next-morning delivery is free if Lisa orders by 2 pm. We used to have problems with delivery years ago, missing pieces, late trucks, so Ray started picking up himself and it just stuck. I think delivery is fine now, Lisa uses it for parts every day. For installs we just never switched back.

**Interviewer:** What does Ray's crew do while he's at Northgate?

**SME:** They meet at the job and do prep, pull the old equipment if the homeowner's ready, or they wait. It depends. On a two-system job the crew is usually standing around for some of that time.

## [06:00] Install day

**Interviewer:** Walk me through an install day.

**SME:** Crew lead checks the folder: proposal, equipment, permit, any notes from Nadia about the house. They do the install, start up the system, and write the readings on the start-up sheet. Then the photos. Then the walk-through with the homeowner, thermostat and filter, and the customer signs the completion form.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the photos.

**SME:** The procedure says before leaving, the crew lead photographs the old equipment, the new equipment, and the data plate on every new unit, the model and serial number, and texts them to me. I print them for the folder. And the procedure says no job is closed without data plate photos.

**Interviewer:** Is that how it works?

**SME:** Honestly, a lot of the time the photos don't happen. If the crew is running late, it's 6 pm, the homeowner wants them out, they skip it. Or they take the new unit but not the data plate. I'd say most weeks I'm missing photos on a few jobs. And I close the job anyway, because the folder has to go to Dana for the balance invoice. I'm not going to hold up the money for a photo.

**Interviewer:** What happens to the missing photos?

**SME:** I tell myself I'll get the serial number later. Sometimes a tech is out there on a tune-up and I ask him to snap it. Sometimes I call the homeowner and ask them to read it to me, which is not great. Sometimes it just doesn't get done.

## [09:30] Warranty registration

**Interviewer:** Why do you need the serial numbers?

**SME:** For warranty registration on the Aerovance dealer portal. If the unit is registered within 60 days of install, the homeowner gets a 10-year parts warranty. If not, it's the base 5 years. I register them from the data plate photos, model and serial for each unit.

**Interviewer:** When do you do the registrations?

**SME:** I try to do them in a batch at the end of each week, folders with photos go in a pile on my desk. In busy weeks it's every two or three weeks. The example in the procedure, the Ostrowski job in Gahanna, JOB-2026-0907, installed September 15th, I registered it on the 29th. That's a good one.

**Interviewer:** How many didn't get registered in time this year?

**SME:** When I went through the folders for the year, 37 of the 310 residential installs were not registered within 60 days. Most of those are ones where I never got the serial number, or the folder went to Dana and I didn't have the photos. Nadia has a login too, but she's selling, it's not her job.

**Interviewer:** Does that cost the company anything?

**SME:** It does when a part fails. If the unit is past the base warranty, or the registration's missing and Aerovance won't cover it, we usually eat the part to keep the customer happy. This year that was five claims, about $4,200 in parts we paid for. And it's going to get worse as those 37 units get older, because the homeowner thinks they have 10 years.

## [12:00] Permits and rooftop units

**Interviewer:** How do permits work?

**SME:** I pull them online from the city or county site, pay with the company card, and print the permit for the folder. Each town is a little different. It's about 15 minutes per job once you know the site. Inspections I schedule by phone, and the homeowner has to be home, so that's another call.

**Interviewer:** And the rooftop units?

**SME:** Wes sells and prices those, I schedule them. I need the crane, the curb adapter if the new unit doesn't match the old curb, and a tech for the controls. The equipment for RTUs Lisa orders from Northgate too, and those get delivered to our yard because they don't fit in Ray's truck. Forty this year. The data plates on RTUs are up on the roof, so those photos get skipped even more.

## [13:30] Problems and callbacks

**Interviewer:** What else goes wrong on installs?

**SME:** Equipment not at Northgate in the morning. Then Ray calls Lisa, the crew does prep, and Lisa finds the unit at another branch and Ray drives there. That's maybe once or twice a month. Inspections sometimes fail, usually something small, and I book the fix and the reinspection. And callbacks within 30 days, Sam or the crew lead goes back at no charge.

**Interviewer:** How are callbacks tracked?

**SME:** In the install log, a column for callbacks. Kyle looks at it at month-end. I'm not sure how it gets into the Monthly Numbers, Dana does that.

**Interviewer:** Is there a checklist for the end of the job?

**SME:** The completion form the customer signs, and the folder has a sheet with boxes: start-up readings, photos, walk-through, warranty registration. The warranty box is the one that's checked last, because I do it after. A lot of folders go to Dana with that box empty, and then they're in her file cabinet.

## [16:00] Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** How do you work with Nadia?

**SME:** Good. She tells me about the house before the crew gets there, tight attic, dog, whatever. Her proposals are clear about what equipment. Sometimes she sells a job for a date I don't have open, and we move it, but that's normal.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing about installs?

**SME:** Get the photos and the serial numbers every time, and the registration done without it sitting in my pile. And maybe have Northgate deliver the equipment the night before so Ray is on the job at 7:30 with his crew instead of at the counter. I'd have to talk to Lisa and Kyle about that.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**SME:** The crews are good. Most of what goes wrong is paperwork, not the install.

**Interviewer:** Thanks, Ben.
