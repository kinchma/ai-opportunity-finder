# Service Ticket to Invoice - Interview with Dana Pruitt

Interviewee: Dana Pruitt, Office Manager and Bookkeeper (since 2008)
Date: 2026-10-28
Fictional transcript. About 19 minutes. Timestamps are approximate.

## [00:00] Role

**Interviewer:** Dana, you have a lot of roles here. How would you describe your job?

**SME:** Officially I'm the office manager and bookkeeper. I do QuickBooks, so invoices, payments, bills, the bank, and the month-end close. I report to Ruth. Unofficially I'm also IT. If the printer jams, the phones go down, or somebody can't get into email, they come to me. I've been here since 2008, so I've just kind of become the person who knows where everything is.

**Interviewer:** How much of your week goes to the IT side?

**SME:** It depends on the week. Some weeks nothing, some weeks a whole day. When the phone system went down in August I was on with the phone company for most of a morning, and nothing got entered that day. In the role matrix I think we put it at about 10% of my time. It's the interruptions more than the hours. I'll be halfway through a stack of tickets and somebody needs the printer fixed.

**Interviewer:** Who would handle it if you weren't here?

**SME:** Kyle, I guess, or they'd wait for me. There's a guy we call for the server once or twice a year. Nobody else really knows the passwords and the vendor contacts. I keep them in a notebook in my desk.

## [03:00] The tickets

**Interviewer:** Let's talk about service tickets. Where does a ticket start?

**SME:** With the tech. Every job gets a three-part carbon ticket. The tech writes the customer, address, equipment, what he did, parts with part numbers and quantity, his hours, the price, what payment he took, and the customer signs. White copy to the customer, yellow to me, pink stays in his van book.

**Interviewer:** How do the yellow copies get to you?

**SME:** There's a tray by my door. Techs are supposed to drop them at the end of the day. If they go straight home from the last job, which is a lot of them, I get them at the Monday morning meeting. So a Thursday job might reach me on Monday.

**Interviewer:** And then you type them in.

**SME:** Yes. I sort them by date and tech, check each one against the dispatch spreadsheet, the ones Trevor has marked Done, and then I type each one into QuickBooks. Customer, the items, labor hours, the parts from the price list, the Comfort Club discount if they're a member, and the payment if they paid on site.

**Interviewer:** How long does that take per ticket?

**SME:** About four minutes on average. Longer when I can't read the writing, or there's no part number and I have to figure out which capacitor he means. Some guys write beautifully. Some guys, I have to call them.

## [06:30] Volume and timing

**Interviewer:** How many tickets a day?

**SME:** About 22 on a normal day. That's the 5,200 service calls a year plus the 290 commercial PM tickets. In a heat wave it's 35 or more, and that's when I fall behind. Then the techs are also bringing them late because they're working late, so it piles up.

**Interviewer:** So on a normal day that's about an hour and a half of typing?

**SME:** About that, on a good day. More like two hours with the phone calls to techs and the lookups. In July it was easily three hours some days, and that's when the rest of my work waits.

**Interviewer:** What about customers who pay on site?

**SME:** About 75% of residential customers pay the tech right there, card on the reader on his phone or a check. For those I apply the payment and then match it to the bank deposit later. The card reader report and the tickets don't always line up by day, so I match them by amount and name.

**Interviewer:** And the ones you invoice?

**SME:** Commercial customers and the property managers. I create the invoice and mail or email it. Brookfield Pike Properties wants a PDF with our ticket number on every invoice, so theirs I email one at a time. On average a commercial invoice goes out about eight days after the job. Some of that is the ticket getting to me, some is me getting to it.

## [10:00] An example

**Interviewer:** Can you walk me through one?

**SME:** Sure, here's one from September. SC-2026-4102, September 3rd, Kevin Amadi at Brookfield Pike building 7. He replaced a condenser fan motor and wrote 1.5 hours. It was a Thursday, and Kevin went straight home, and Friday he was out. So I got the yellow copy Tuesday the 8th. I entered it on the 10th as INV-2026-1877, and emailed it on the 11th. That's eight days, which is about normal for us.

**Interviewer:** Does Brookfield pay on time?

**SME:** They pay on 30 days from the invoice, so a late invoice is late money. They're good about it otherwise.

## [12:00] Missing tickets and checks

**Interviewer:** What happens when a ticket goes missing?

**SME:** It happens every month. I compare the Done calls in the spreadsheet with what I have. Any Done call with no ticket, I ask the tech to bring the pink copy. Usually 25 to 35 tickets a month are late or missing when I first check. Almost all of them turn up. A few a year we just never find, and those don't get billed, or the tech writes it again from memory.

**Interviewer:** Is there a weekly check?

**SME:** Every Friday I run the open invoices report in QuickBooks and compare it to the Done rows for the week. Anything Done with no invoice or payment gets a sticky note on the tech's mailbox. That's where most of the missing ones get found.

**Interviewer:** What about parts that aren't on the price list?

**SME:** I ask Lisa for the cost and, for commercial, I ask Wes for the markup, because the commercial markup is in his pricing sheet and I don't have that. If Wes is out, the invoice waits.

## [13:30] New customers, members and discounts

**Interviewer:** What about new customers? Are they already in QuickBooks when the ticket comes in?

**SME:** No. Marisol looks them up in QuickBooks when they call, but if they're new she only puts them in the dispatch spreadsheet. So when the ticket comes to me I create the customer in QuickBooks from the ticket and the spreadsheet row. That's another couple of minutes, and sometimes the spelling on the ticket and the spreadsheet don't match, so I pick one.

**Interviewer:** And the Comfort Club discount?

**SME:** The tech is supposed to circle "member" on the ticket and take 15% off the repair. Sometimes he forgets, and the customer calls me after, and I issue a credit. A few times a month. I check the member list in the Comfort Club tab when I'm not sure, same list the CSRs use.

**Interviewer:** How do you handle a ticket where the tech collected a check?

**SME:** The check comes in with the ticket, stapled, or sometimes it's in the van for a week. I apply the payment in QuickBooks and make a deposit twice a week. If a check bounces, I call the customer and put the invoice back open. That's rare, maybe four or five a year.

**Interviewer:** Does anyone else look at the tickets after you?

**SME:** Kyle looks at callbacks, the jobs where we had to go back. He pulls those from the file cabinet by hand when he wants to see what the tech wrote the first time. That's the only other use. Otherwise the yellow copies go in the cabinet by month and that's it.

## [16:00] Housecall Pro and ideas

**Interviewer:** Was Housecall Pro supposed to change this?

**SME:** That was the whole point. In 2024 Kyle bought it so the techs would do tickets in the app and it would sync to QuickBooks. I'd stop typing. The techs didn't like it, and after a few weeks they went back to paper. Now it just sends reminder texts. I'm not sure if it even syncs with QuickBooks Desktop, I think it might only do QuickBooks Online. I never set that part up.

**Interviewer:** If you could fix one thing in this process, what would it be?

**SME:** Getting the ticket information to me the same day without me typing it. I don't mind checking. I mind retyping. It's about 5,500 tickets a year at four minutes each, that's a lot of my year. And it would help month-end, because month-end waits on the last tickets.

**Interviewer:** Anything else I should know?

**SME:** Just that if I'm out, nothing gets entered. Ruth can do some of it, but she has payroll. We've never really had a backup for me on the books.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Dana.
