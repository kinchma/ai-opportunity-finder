# Service Call Intake

Owner: Marisol Quintero (CSR). Also Jolene Fairweather. Last updated March 2023.

## Purpose

Every request for service gets into the dispatch spreadsheet the same day with the right customer, address, equipment and problem, so Trevor can put it on the board.

## Where requests come from

- Phone calls during office hours (7:30 am to 5:00 pm, Monday to Friday). Most of our volume. About 5,200 service calls a year come in this way or from the sources below.
- After-hours calls go to Midstate Answering. They take a message and email it to service@. For no-heat or no-cool calls they also text the on-call tech. About 1,050 messages a year, more in January and July.
- The website request form. It sends an email to service@ with the name, phone, address and message. About 410 a year.
- Email and Facebook messages now and then.

## Steps for a phone call

1. Look up the customer in QuickBooks (customer list). If new, take name, address, phone, email.
2. Ask what equipment they have and the problem. Ask if they are a Comfort Club member (check the member list in the Comfort Club tab of the dispatch spreadsheet).
3. Quote the $99 service call fee (no charge for the visit if the repair is done; members get 15% off repairs).
4. Add a row to "Dispatch 2026.xlsx": date, customer, address, phone, equipment, problem, member Y/N, priority (no heat or no cool = P1).
5. Tell the customer Trevor will call with a time window, or give a window if Trevor is at the desk.

## Steps for after-hours messages

1. First thing in the morning (7:30), open service@ and print the Midstate messages from the night.
2. Check which ones the on-call tech already handled (call the on-call tech or check his texts with Trevor).
3. Type each remaining message into the dispatch spreadsheet as a new row and call the customer back to confirm.
4. File the printouts in the "Answering Service" folder.

On a busy winter Monday there can be 15 to 25 messages from the weekend. Average is about 4 per morning.

## Steps for web form requests

1. Check service@ during the day when the phones are quiet.
2. Call the customer back. If no answer, leave a message and reply to the email.
3. Once you reach them, enter the call like a phone call.

Midstate message, 2026-01-12 02:14: "Patricia Hollins, 418 Quarry Bend, Westerville, furnace blowing cold air, has small kids, callback 614-555-0187." On-call tech Derek Hsu went out at 3:10 am (P1). In the morning Marisol typed it in as row 2026-0112-07 so the ticket could be matched, and added a follow-up call.

Web form, 2026-09-18 16:40: "AC making a grinding noise, not urgent." Called back 2026-09-21 morning, booked for 2026-09-23 as call SC-2026-4418.

## When things go wrong

- Duplicate rows happen when a customer calls and also emails. Search the spreadsheet by phone number before adding.
- If Midstate's email does not arrive, call them for the message log.
- If a customer says nobody called back on a web request, apologize, book them first, and tell Kyle.
